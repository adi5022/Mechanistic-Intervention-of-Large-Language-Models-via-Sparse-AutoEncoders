### 3.3 The ROME chapter (what it says; important for the paper)
- ROME (Meng et al. 2022) = closed-form rank-one edit of one MLP matrix; official code vendored at `third_party/rome` (commit 0874014) by a teammate on branch `rome-factual-editing` on 2026-10-07; environment in `src/rome_baseline`, `scripts/rome/00..07`.
- Causal tracing on GPT-2 Small over 97 known facts (342 of 1,209 known facts are correct; traced those with p >= 0.2): strongest effect at the last token in layers 8 to 11 (residual 0.32 at layer 11, attention up to 0.10 at layer 10); no sharp mid-layer MLP site at the last subject token (unlike GPT-2 XL in the ROME paper). Consistent with, not proof of, our edit layer 8 and the layer plateau 6 to 9.
- Layer sweep (layers 1,3,5,7,9; 100 CounterFact dev records, seed 0): efficacy 100% everywhere; neighbourhood score falls 0.716 (no edit) to 0.657, 0.663, 0.629, 0.528, 0.534; best overall S = 0.851 at layer 3 (layers 1 to 5 within noise); about 4 s per edit on CPU.
- **ROME's three scores:** ES (new answer more probable than true, edit prompt), PS (same on reworded prompts), NS (true answer still more probable on neighbour prompts); S = harmonic mean. Our "rewordings" and "neighbours" in the transfer study are exactly PS and NS; the 100 ROME dev records (`random.Random(0).sample(range(len(ds)),100)`) are excluded from all our test sets (`src/transfer/benchmark.py`). Our success criterion (rank 1 over the whole vocabulary) is stricter than ES.
- **Cross-check (indicative only, different record sets):** ROME layer 3 lifts PS 33% to 98.5%, NS 72% to 66%; our gradient-descent edit on 88 test records lifts PS 40% to 67%, NS 63% to 54%. Agrees with "our edit is a push".
- **Base rates:** 342/1209 (28%) ROME known facts; 15/45 (33%) our first eligibility sample; 1,817/21,913 (8.3%) CounterFact prompts already rank 1.
- **Not done:** ROME plan steps 6 to 8 (frozen shared benchmark, adapter for our editors, results table). The head-to-head is therefore NOT in the report and must not be claimed. What it would need: one frozen record list disjoint from the 100 ROME dev records, one scoring script (ES/PS/NS + rank 1 + same-prompt KL), random-word control, pass bars written first, timings on one machine. ## 6. The D6 discussion (needed for the presentation)

- **What D6 is:** dials (multipliers of Small's 200 candidate SAE features) are tuned by gradient descent through the linear translator against **Medium's** last-position output; the translated change `dose * (delta @ W)` is added to Medium's layer 16. Medium needs no SAE. Code: `src/transfer/b_aware_edit.py: tune_through_translator`; tool `tools/transfer/11_b_aware_edit.py`; data `packs/cross_model_transfer/d6_b_aware.json`.
- **"Ceiling" explained to the user:** the best result a method can reach when given the most help. For the *amortised transfer* question (edit once in Small, never look at Medium), D6 is the ceiling: 95% vs 8 to 20%. So the channel can carry a rank-1 edit; the limit is that an edit tuned for Small does not suit Medium.
- **The user's correction (valid):** D6 is also a method in its own right, "steering a model that has no SAE through another model's SAE features connected by a linear map", and "looking at the result" is exactly what the main project's gradient descent does. So present D6 as a result, not only as a ceiling. Present two questions: (1) amortised transfer: 8 to 20%; (2) borrowed-SAE steering: 95%.
- **Caveats that remain either way:** random-word target reaches 83% (controllability, not fact transfer); random directions with the same firing pattern reach 81% (Small's SAE adds about 14 points); no free-vector baseline in Medium (an optimised vector of the same size without any SAE) was ever run, so "SAE features give Medium a better control panel" is unproven; Gate 5 failed by 3 points on neighbours (8 vs 5 allowed) and the bar was stricter than the source edit's own 9-point loss in Small (noted afterwards, a post-hoc reading); D6 as a method has no pre-registered bar.
- **Suggested slide wording:** "Can a model with no SAE be steered through another model's SAE features connected by a linear map? Tuned against Medium's output, Small's 200 features reach the target on 95% of records; specific to the record (another record's recipe 0%); neighbour damage about the same as the edit in Small. Random-word targets reach 83% and random directions 81%, so this shows controllability, not fact transfer. Edits made in Small and carried across without looking at Medium reach 8 to 20%."
- **Expected questions:** "isn't 95% just forcing the answer?" (yes in part; random word 83%); "why not report 95% as the transfer result?" (Medium's output used per sentence); "what did the SAE contribute?" (95 vs 81).

Glossary used with the user: Export (Small to Medium), Import (Medium state into Small), translator, rank 1/top-1, dose, gate (pass bar fixed before the run), push vs fact, control, recipe (feature ids + multipliers), relay mode.

### 7.3 How a run works (`run_transfer_edit`)
1. Clean context in Small (`build_clean_context`); if the target is already rank 1, a note is shown and nothing runs.
2. Non-Direct: `run_gradient_descent_edit(small, sae, clean, tid, prompt, layer, hook_name, top_n, positions="all", steps, additive=..., add_cap_factor=...)` gives fids, multipliers, and (additive) added amounts. The recipe's change at Small's layer is `delta[p] = sum_k a_k act_k[p] W_dec[k]` (+ additive amounts at the last position), start-of-text row zeroed.
3. Translation: ridge `delta @ W`; neural `f(h+delta) - f(h)`; trained `delta @ W_d7`; all times `dose`; row 0 zeroed; injected into Medium with `run_injected(medium, tokens, tr[None], ml)`. Small's "after" is the same recipe through `run_injected(small, ...)`.
4. **Direct (D6):** candidate features = top-200 active features of Small (`get_top_active_features`); `tune_through_translator(small, medium, sae, W_ridge, fids, tokens, tid, ml, dose, steps)` tunes multipliers against Medium's output; **Small is not optimised**, its box shows what the same dials do inside Small (note under the box). The additive option does not apply to Direct (the run says so and is labelled plain GD).
