# Research Journal Entry 25: Literature check and assessment of the research direction

**Date:** October 1, 2026
**Status:** Completed (search-based sweep; not an exhaustive review; no full paper was read).
**Why it was done:** a potential employer questioned whether the SAE approach is sound; the author asked whether the direction is right, what is novel, and what DeepMind's retreat from SAEs means for this project.
**Full note with all sources and how well each was checked:** `docs/literature_review_sae_editing.md`.

---

## 1. Summary

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is this an established field? | **Yes.** Fact editing (ROME, MEMIT), SAE steering (SAE-TS, SpARE, SAE "output feature" selection), SAE feature ablation for unlearning, and in-context editing (IKE) all have prior work. |
| Which ideas here are novel? | None conceptually. **Possibly novel as a combination and use case:** joint mute-and-boost on one fact with a per-candidate strict safety filter, cumulative sweep and pool refill, pushing a single fact to rank 1 on GPT-2 small; the dynamic filter study; the planned learned strengths. No exact match found; the search was limited to web search results. |
| What did DeepMind find? | On detecting harmful intent in new prompts, dense linear probes (AUROC about 0.999) beat SAE probes, which stayed distinctly worse out of distribution even with k-sparse probes or fine-tuned SAEs. The team de-prioritised SAE work (model diffing, model organisms of deception and thinking models instead) and says SAEs are not useless and not a game-changer. |
| What else points the same way? | AxBench: on concept steering SAEs scored about 0.165 overall against prompting 0.894 and DiffMean 0.239. A study on the same GPT-2 SAE family found they "struggle to reach the neuron baseline" on a factual-knowledge task. A paper on post-intervention recovery found suppressed behaviour could be restored through the SAE reconstruction residual. |
| What points the other way? | Arad et al.: selecting features by their causal effect on outputs makes SAE steering 2 to 3 times better and competitive with supervised methods; the project's selector is of this kind. |
| Should the author be worried? | Not enough to stop; enough to change how the work is framed. The use case differs from the studies above (single-fact next-token flip, small base model) but is not exempt from their findings. |

## 2. What follows (decisions recorded)
1. **Compare fairly** on the same prompts, timed on one machine: the SAE editor, IKE (GPT-2 is not instruction tuned, so in-context demonstrations), ROME, DiffMean; existing implementations only (EasyEdit, AxBench) in a separate environment (EXP-015).
2. **Test more than "rank 1 on the same sentence"**: CounterFact supplies paraphrase and neighbourhood prompts for checking generalisation and spread.
3. **Document tried ideas whether or not they work** (the learned-strength study, Entry 23).
4. **Do not argue for or against the SAE approach in advance**; let the comparison decide. The author's experience that the strengths change outcomes is a reason to test the learned-strength variant, not a reason to skip the comparison.
