# Research Journal Entry 6: Shared Feature Aggregation & Clamping in Weighted Reduction

**Date:** July 25, 2026  
**Status:** Experiment H10 Completed and Analyzed  

---

## 1. Layman's Explanation (The Aggregation Bottleneck)

In our previous attempt at **Weighted Multi-Competitor Reduction** (reducing the power of all competitor tokens ranked above the target), we observed that the target was still blocked. We discovered that multiple competitors "collapsed" onto the same few features (e.g. Feature 313 driven by `" the"`, `" a"`, and `" an"`).

Under the initial design, when multiple competitors shared a feature, we only kept the maximum computed strength:
* `" the"` wanted Feature 313 muted at `0.284`.
* `" a"` wanted Feature 313 muted at `0.166`.
* `" an"` wanted Feature 313 muted at `0.041`.
* **Result**: We muted Feature 313 at `0.284`.

In **Experiment H10**, we tested whether this under-muted the shared features. We replaced the `max()` selection with additive accumulation (`sum()`), allowing the competitors to pool their threat values, before applying a ceiling clamp at `max_strength = 0.70`.
* **Accumulated Result**: Feature 313 muted at `0.284 + 0.166 + 0.041 = 0.492`.

## 3. Scientific Conclusions

* **Rejection of Hypothesis H10**: Experiment H10 rejects the hypothesis that the weak performance of Weighted Multi-Competitor Reduction is primarily caused by using maximum aggregation for shared SAE features.
* **Interpretation of Results**:
  - Increasing the intervention strength of the strongest identified shared feature (Feature 313) by approximately 73% (from `0.284` to `0.492`) produced only a marginal increase in the target probability (from `1.29%` to `1.39%`) and no rank improvement (remained at Rank 8).
  - This demonstrates that the strongest identified SAE feature alone is insufficient to substantially suppress these competitors.
  - One possible explanation is that these competitors derive support from multiple influential SAE features rather than a single dominant feature. However, this has not yet been experimentally verified, motivating the next phase of research (Experiment H11).
