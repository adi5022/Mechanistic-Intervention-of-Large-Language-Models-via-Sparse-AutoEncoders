# Research Journal Entry 26: Learned strengths, trained on the prefix proxy: a better global pair, little gain from choosing per prompt

**Date:** October 2 to 3, 2026
**Branch:** `pool-refill-implementation`
**Status:** Training and proxy evaluation COMPLETED. The real sweep (the actual system) has NOT been run with any of these strengths, so every success number below is from a stand-in (the "prefix proxy"), not from the real system.
**Plan:** Entry 23. **Data:** Entry 24. **Plain-language session report:** `docs/session_report_2026-09-30_to_10-02.md`.

## 1. Summary

| Question | Answer (prefix proxy, held-out prompts) |
|---|---|
| Does a better choice of the mute and boost strengths fix more prompts than fixed 0.6 / 0.5? | **Yes, clearly.** On the 300 test prompts (validation excluded) fixed 0.6 / 0.5 reached rank 1 on 75 (25.0%); a learned fixed pair (mute 0.98, boost 1.81) on 116 (38.7%). Paired: 42 prompts only the learned pair fixed, 1 only the reference fixed (exact McNemar p < 0.0001). |
| Does choosing the strengths per prompt (PromptNet) beat the best single pair? | **Not shown.** PromptNet reached 119 of the 300 test prompts (39.7%): 3 prompts more than the pair and none fewer (exact McNemar p = 0.25). On all 450 held-out prompts: 4 prompts more, 0 fewer (p = 0.125). |
| How close is the per-prompt tuned upper bound ("oracle")? | 123 of 300 test prompts (41.0%); 12 prompts more than the learned pair and 5 fewer (p = 0.14 on test only; p = 0.027 on all 450). It uses lower strengths (mean mute 0.83, boost 1.10). |
| What does the better pair cost? | **Side effects roughly double**: mean KL of prompts that reach rank 1 rose from 0.156 (reference) to 0.38 (learned pair, PromptNet), 0.36 (oracle). This depends on the side-effect weight lam_kl = 10, which was my choice. |
| Where do the gains come from? | Mostly prompts that start at rank 6 to 100. Prompts that start at rank 101 to 1000 stay almost unfixable (reference 1 of 117, learned pair 6 of 117, oracle 8 of 117). |
| What is NOT shown? | The real sweep's success with these strengths, the real system's side effects, anything beyond GPT-2 small layer 8, and how the result changes with the side-effect weight. |

## 2. What was run
`tools/train_strength_models.py` (defaults: sizes 100, 250, 500, 1000; 3 seeds; up to 60 epochs, early stopping patience 8; lam_kl 10, lam_size 0.02, margin 0.3; training prefix grid 1, 3, 8, 21, all; evaluation grid 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, 55, all; oracle 100 steps) on the RTX 4050 laptop (torch 2.5.1+cu121), 1,195 s in total. Output files: `docs/Research_Journal/packs/strength_models/` (`results.json`, `strengths_export.json`, `promptnet_best.pt`, `analysis_output.txt`). The analysis in Section 3 was produced by `tools/analyse_strength_models.py`, which re-evaluates the exported per-prompt strengths on the local cache and tables (numbers match the training printout, e.g. validation 63 of 150 for the selected PromptNet).
Built-in checks passed on the laptop: reduced forward pass vs full model 1.1e-05; table-based edit vs the real hook at 0.6 / 0.5 on 5 prompts 1.7e-05 with the same target rank (5 of 5); baseline reproduced to 8.9e-08; allowed features at 0.6 / 0.5 against the real sweep's round 0: identical (ids and order up to near-ties) except for one feature with an effect of 5.8e-06 (numerical noise).

## 4. Interpretation
1. **The main finding is a better global default**: mute near 0.98 and boost near 1.8, instead of 0.6 and 0.5, under this proxy. That matches the outcome the plan called "a better default pair was found; per-prompt adaptation is not shown" (Entry 23, section 8.4).
2. **Per-prompt choice added at most about one point** (3 to 4 prompts), not distinguishable from zero with 300 to 450 prompts. A larger test set, a richer input than 12 summary numbers, or per-feature strengths (version 2) might show more; this study cannot say.
3. **The learned pair sits at the edge of what the tables allow** (mute 0.98 of 1.0, boost 1.81 of 2.0, and the network's boost often reaches 2.0). The best setting may lie beyond the tested range; the tables stop there, so this cannot be checked without extending them.
4. **The gain has a price**: about twice the side effects. The side-effect weight (lam_kl = 10) decides where on the success versus side-effect curve the training lands; the curve itself has not been mapped. A gentler pair that fixes fewer prompts but disturbs other predictions less would also be a "better default" by a different criterion.
5. **Hard prompts (starting rank 101 to 1000) remain almost unfixable** under every strength choice tried, consistent with the headroom study (Entry 24), where even free per-feature multipliers reached only about half of them without a damage limit.

## 5. Limitations
- **Everything here is the proxy**: growing prefixes of the allowed features in the sweep's order, in a grid of sizes (so it can miss successes at intermediate sizes), with no pool refill and no step limit. The real sweep can fix prompts the proxy cannot, and its side effects are smaller because it stops at the first success. These numbers do not predict the real success rate; whether the strengths transfer to the real sweep is the open question.
- The side-effect numbers are of prompts that succeed, measured at the first successful prefix; failures are not compared.
- 150 prompts per test set, templated relations (CounterFact), one base model and layer; the unseen-relation set contains only 5 relations.
- Choices made by the author of the code, not tuned systematically: lam_kl, lam_size, margin 0.3, the prefix grids, the strength ranges, the network size (12 to 32 to 32 to 2), learning rates (the control has its own, larger).
- Validation was used for early stopping and seed selection; test numbers are therefore the clean ones. No hyperparameter search was run.
