# Research Journal Entry 31: Cross-model transfer, part 1: a translator between GPT-2 small and GPT-2 medium, the country-swap control, and a pilot on a real edit

**Date:** October 9 to 10, 2026

## 1. Why
The project edits GPT-2 small so that it answers a chosen word, with no change to any weight. The new question: can the change an edit makes inside one model be sent into a second model, at inference time, so that the second model answers differently too? Two directions, named by where the change is read and where it is written:
- **Export (small to medium):** the edit is made in GPT-2 small, where the SAE and all the tools are; its change is translated and added to GPT-2 medium.
- **Import (medium to small):** medium's internal state (it knows more facts) is translated and added to small, so small answers better.

Two things block a naive version. The two models do not share internal coordinates (small is 768 wide, medium 1024; a feature number in one means nothing in the other), so a translator is needed. And Entry 30 showed that "the answer word reaches rank 1" is weak evidence (the additive edit does it for random words), so every test here carries controls.

## 2. Method (all new code; nothing in the older files was edited)
- **Translator:** a linear map fitted on the residual stream directly (not on SAE features), by ridge regression from running sums, on wikitext-2 text both models read. Hook point `blocks.L.hook_resid_pre`; small layers 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 against medium layers 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, fitted in both directions. The first token of each sequence is skipped. The exact solution is used because for a linear map under squared error it is what gradient descent converges to (checked, section 3.2).
- **Checks of the translator (A2):** (1) stitching: run the receiver with its own state replaced by the translated one and compare next-word loss with the untouched model, with a destroyed state (the average state), and with the translation of a different text; (2) differences: translate the difference between two sentences that differ in one word and compare it with the receiver's real difference (cosine), with chance levels from other pairs' differences; 6 templates, 210 pairs, measured at the word and at the last position.
- **Country-swap control (B1):** for two countries (a, b) that the receiver answers correctly ("The capital of France is the city of" gives Paris), add the translated difference (state for b minus state for a, every position, times a dose) to the receiver while it reads the sentence for a. Success = the swapped capital becomes the top answer. Arms: translated; the receiver's own real difference (ceiling); random vector, random linear map and a different pair's difference, each the same size at each position. Dose chosen on 30% of the country pairs, numbers on the other 70%. Gate 2 was written into the plan before the run (section 4).
- **Pilot (D):** 30 CounterFact records with single-token counterfactual targets (ROME dev records excluded; records where either model already answers with the target excluded); the project's unchanged gradient-descent edit in small (200 candidates, 100 steps); its change at every position, `sum_k a_k act_k W_dec[k]`; translated with small 8 to medium 12 or 16 and added to medium at doses 1 to 4. Controls: random vector, another record's translated change, and a push along the target word's own output direction in medium.

## 3. Results
### 3.1 A0, setup (all checks passed)
Both models load; vocabularies identical (50,257); identical token ids on 200 wikitext sentences; TransformerLens agrees with the plain Hugging Face model to 1.1e-4 and 1.2e-4 in log-probability; both models and a batch fit on the RTX 4050 together (peak 4.18 GB of 6.4 GB). wikitext-2: 2,391,884 training tokens in 18,833 sequences, 247,289 validation tokens in 1,947 sequences (128 tokens each).

### 3.2 A1, the translators (500,126 fitting tokens, 100,076 held-out; ridge strength chosen on half A, numbers from half B)
Per-dimension R-squared (every dimension counts equally; raw R-squared is dominated by the largest dimensions): small 8 to medium 16 0.581, small 8 to medium 12 0.592, medium 16 to small 8 0.661, medium 12 to small 8 0.666. Best of the grid: small 2 to medium 4 0.646, medium 4 to small 2 0.729 (early layers mostly carry which word it is, which is easy to predict). Medium to small is easier than small to medium by 0.03 to 0.08 at matched depths. None of the 50 maps chose an edge value of the ridge grid. Gradient-descent check on one pair with 60,000 tokens: 0.6287 against 0.6289 for the exact solution (0.6306 with the best ridge strength), so nothing is gained by training. A fit with 20,000 tokens (quick mode) picked the strongest ridge everywhere, as expected with so few data.

### 3.3 A2, do the translators work? (100 unseen text sequences; clean next-word loss small 4.245, medium 3.964)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| small 2 to medium 4 | 3.96 / 4.13 / 10.70 / 10.88 | 0.976 | 0.709 | 0.294 (0.242; 70%) |
| small 6 to medium 8 | 3.96 / 4.15 / 9.12 / 10.76 | 0.964 | 0.656 | 0.269 (0.209; 75%) |
| small 8 to medium 12 | 3.96 / 4.20 / 8.49 / 10.68 | 0.947 | 0.648 | 0.242 (0.199; 65%) |
| small 8 to medium 16 | 3.96 / 4.36 / 8.17 / 10.53 | 0.906 | 0.628 | 0.275 (0.202; 83%) |
| medium 4 to small 2 | 4.24 / 4.37 / 10.52 / 10.85 | 0.980 | 0.762 | 0.279 (0.221; 74%) |
| medium 8 to small 6 | 4.24 / 4.42 / 8.22 / 10.59 | 0.957 | 0.711 | 0.308 (0.238; 73%) |

The translated state keeps the receiver working (91 to 98% of the lost loss recovered on all eight pairs) and is specific to the text (the wrong text is worse than destroying the state). The translated difference between two sentences carries which word changed well (0.63 to 0.76) but the downstream summary state at the last position only weakly (0.24 to 0.31), above chance on every pair and noisy at the level of single pairs. Translated differences are 0.44 to 0.96 times the size of the real ones, so the dose has to be above 1. Gate 1 as proposed (recovered at least 0.8 and last-position cosine at least 0.3 and above the within-template chance 95th percentile): 2 of 8 pairs pass, both medium to small (16 to 8 and 8 to 6); small to medium is above chance on all four pairs but under the 0.3 line (best 0.294). The 0.3 line was a proposal made before the data and was not moved. SAE feature directions of small layer 8 keep a cosine of 0.89 after a round trip small to medium (layer 12 or 16) to small, against 0.81 for random directions: features survive somewhat better than random directions, which is modest.

### 3.4 B1, the country swap (Gate 2 PASSES in both directions)
Test cases are ordered country pairs the receiver answers correctly on both sentences (two wordings of the prompt; 28 countries for medium, 25 for small).

| | Export: small 8 to medium 16 (1,021 test cases) | Import: medium 16 to small 8 (695 test cases) |
|---|---|---|
| swapped capital becomes the top answer, all positions (dose chosen on dev) | **55%** (dose 3) | **33%** (dose 1.5) |
| at the word's position only / at the last position only | 60% / 1% | 41% / 5% |
| random vector / random linear map / another pair's difference (each at its best dose) | 0% / 0% / 1% | 0% / 0% / 1% |
| receiver's own real difference, dose 1, all positions | 100% | 100% |
| median rank of the swapped capital: unchanged / translated / random | 1431 / 1 / 980 | 2351 / 5 / 1574 |
| top answer when it is not the swapped capital: original capital / another capital / not a capital | 1% / 4% / 41% | 0% / 1% / 65% |
| 12 unrelated prompts, translated last-position vector vs random of the same size: KL, top-1 flips | 0.049, 14% vs 0.023, 11% | 0.044, 27% vs 0.027, 25% |

Gate 2 (fixed in the plan before the run): (a) at least 20% at the dev dose, (b) at least 15 points above the best control, (c) paired Wilcoxon on the gain in logit difference p < 0.01 (reported as 0, underflow), sanity checks (zero dose reproduces the unchanged model; the receiver's own difference at dose 1 gives at least 97%): all hold in both directions. The six other layer pairs (exploratory, no gate): translated 79% (small 2 to medium 4), 71% (6 to 8), 67% (8 to 12), 42% (medium 4 to small 2), 38% (8 to 6), 36% (12 to 8); controls at most 1%. The earlier the layer the receiver is fed at, the better it works, which fits the swap being mostly a change in which word was named. Where it fails: in Import 65% of top answers are not in the capital list (mostly first pieces of city names such as `' St'`, `' K'`, `' C'`), so exact top-1 match understates how close the answer gets (median rank 5). The last-position-only variant fails in Import for a reason unrelated to the translator: the small model's own last-position difference also gives 0%.

### 3.5 D pilot (30 records, no split, no gate: a look, not a result)
- The gradient-descent edit reaches rank 1 in small on 17 of the 30 counterfactual targets (median start rank 315, median same-prompt KL 0.92, edit size at the last position 29% of the residual norm).
- **Where the edit's change sits:** 89% of its squared size is at positions between the start token and the last token (the subject and relation words), 5% at the last position. The multiplier edit is mainly a change of the subject's representation, not of the last position.
- **Transfer to medium** (medium's own median rank of the target before anything: 165):

| small 8 to medium 16 | dose 1 | dose 2 | dose 3 | dose 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| translated edit: target is medium's top answer / median rank | 0/30, 25 | 2/30, 21 | 6/30, 23 | 5/30, 89 |
| random vector | 0/30, 159 | 1/30, 261 | 1/30, 378 | 1/30, 560 |
| another record's translated edit | 0/30, 179 | 0/30, 272 | 0/30, 377 | 0/30, 611 |
| push along the target word's own output direction | 5/30, 3 | 26/30, 1 | 28/30, 1 | 30/30, 1 |

(small 8 to medium 12: translated 0, 2, 5, 3 of 30 at doses 1 to 4; word push 0, 6, 17, 26 of 30.) So the translated edit moves the target up in medium about sevenfold in rank (165 to about 22) and makes it the top answer in up to 20% of records, clearly above the random and wrong-record controls, but far below simply pushing the target word's own output direction, which works in almost every record. Medium's top answer is therefore reachable by a trivial push; success at top-1 alone says nothing about facts, and the controlled test must be judged on rewordings, neighbouring facts and unrelated prompts.

## 4. Pre-stated rules (written before the runs they judge)
- Gate 1 (before A2): recovered at least 0.8, last-position difference cosine at least 0.3 and above chance. Result: passes for medium to small, misses for small to medium on the cosine line.
- Gate 2 (before B1): see 3.4. Result: passes in both directions.
- H24 reading for Phase C (not yet run): "association-like" if paraphrase success is clearly above the rank-matched random control (paired test, p < 0.01) and the subject-only edit keeps at least half of the full edit's effect; "read-out steering" if the paraphrase lift is close to the random control or the edit is dominated by the target's output direction; "mixed" otherwise.

## 5. Mistakes made and fixed (so they are not repeated)
1. **A2 sentence templates:** three of the six ended on the changed word, so "last position" was the word itself and the cosine was inflated (about 0.45 instead of about 0.22). All templates now continue past the word. A city template was silently dropped because most cities split into two tokens at the start of a sentence; it was moved mid-sentence.
2. **Tokenizer:** `tokenizer.encode` in this transformers version adds the start token, so every word looked like two tokens; counting must use `add_special_tokens=False`.
3. **B1 prompts:** the plain "The capital of X is" makes both models answer "the" or "a" (small knew no capitals). The wording "...is the city of" forces a city name (small knows 21, medium 27).
4. **A lost run:** the first full B1 run was started with a 50-minute background limit, was killed after four of eight pairs and saved nothing because results were written only at the end. The tool now saves every pair as it finishes, computes only the last position's word scores (identical to 2e-5, much faster: 279 s for the two primary pairs instead of about 19 minutes), and the exploratory pairs run with a smaller dose grid.
5. **A self-test:** the first tolerance was tighter than the statistical floor of its own made-up data (condition number 126); the test now compares with a direct least-squares solve (agreement 3.6e-4) and keeps a loose recovery check.

## 6. What this does and does not show
- Shown: a linear translator fitted on ordinary text between two residual streams keeps the receiving model working; a translated change of "which country the sentence names" flips the receiver's answer far above every control, in both directions; the project's gradient-descent edit puts most of its change at the subject's positions, where the translator is strongest, and a translated edit moves the target up in medium well above random and wrong-record controls.
- NOT shown: that any fact was transferred. B1 sends the difference of two real sentences, not an edit. The pilot has 30 records, no split and no paraphrase, neighbour or unrelated-prompt test, and a trivial word push beats the translated edit. Whether the gradient-descent edit changes a fact or pushes a word (H24) has not been tested.
- Limits: one model pair of one family and one layer grid; test cases in B1 share countries and are not independent; the dose was chosen on pairs that share countries with the test pairs; the translated difference carries the last-position summary weakly (0.24 to 0.31), so edits that live at the last position (the additive edit) are predicted to transfer poorly (a prediction, not a result).

## 7. Decisions and next (not done)
1. **Phase C** (fact edit or word push, GPT-2 small alone): shared benchmark on CounterFact with the paraphrase and neighbour prompts, rank-matched random targets, subject-only and last-only edits, direction of the edit against the target's output direction.
2. **Phase D, controlled export** (proposal in `PLAN.md`; numbers to be confirmed by the author before the run): 50 dev and 150 test counterfactual records disjoint from the pilot and from the ROME dev records, relay mode (the edit's multipliers re-applied to each rewording), the four controls above plus rank-matched random targets, judged on top-1, rank, rewordings, neighbours and unrelated prompts.
3. **Phase E, import:** medium's state translated into small and filtered by small's SAE, target-free arms first.
4. Later: replication on Gemma 3 270M and 1B (both have SAEs), a feature-to-feature view, a tester app.
