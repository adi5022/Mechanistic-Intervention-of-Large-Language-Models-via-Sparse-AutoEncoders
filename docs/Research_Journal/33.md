## 1. Why
Entry 32 ended with the export failing its pre-set bar: the edit tuned in GPT-2 small and carried to GPT-2 medium made the target medium's top answer on only 5 to 8% of records, although its rank improved a lot. Four candidate reasons were listed (a training target that favours common structure; an edit tuned for small's later layers; a translator that never saw edited states; a single injection layer). This entry tests the last one directly (D5), tests whether the channel can carry a rank-1 edit at all (D6), and prepares the test of the first three (D7). It also records what the author's questions made explicit about the method (section 2).

## 2. What exactly was done (plain, as clarified with the author)
- **The edit is the multiplier edit, not the additive one.** In GPT-2 small the project's gradient-descent tool tunes one multiplier per candidate SAE feature (200 features at layer 8, `gpt2-small-res-jb`) until small answers with the counterfactual target (for example " English" instead of " French" for "The mother tongue of Danielle Darrieux is"). It reaches rank 1 in small on 116 of 200 benchmark records (58%); the main numbers use the 88 test records where it did. The additive edit (Entry 30; it reaches rank 1 on every prompt but also on 20 of 20 random words, and writes at 126 to 192% of the residual norm) was **not** used and its transfer is **untested** (Entry 31 predicted it would transfer worse, because it sits at the last position where the translator is weakest; that is a prediction).
- **What is injected.** The change the edit makes to small's layer-8 state at every position (edited minus unedited), translated by the linear map (never the whole state), times a dose, added to medium's residual stream at layer 12 and/or 16 while it reads the same sentence. No weight is changed in either model.
- **No SAE on medium.** The recipe (feature ids and multipliers) comes from small's SAE. What was tuned on medium's side: the translator was fitted on medium's activations over ordinary text (wikitext, no edits), and two scalars (dose and injection layer) were chosen from medium's outputs on 50 separate dev records; an outcome-free norm-matched dose agreed. The 150 test records were never used for any choice.
- **The rank numbers.** On the 88 test records, medium's median rank of the target is 142 before anything. (165 was the median on the earlier 30-record pilot.) After the injection the median is 14 (layer 16) or 11 (layers 12 and 16). It is a median over records, not true for every record; the target becomes top-1 on only 5 to 8%.

Why it does nothing (seed 0 training curves, Figure 8): the training shortfall from rank 1 on the tuned prompts (the hinge, averaged over the tuned prompts) falls from about 4.2 logits at the start to about 2.4 at lambda 0, about 1.1 at lambda 0.1 and about 0.15 at lambda 1.0: the map learns to put the 289 training targets on top. Caveats: lambda-star was chosen by the dev score as fixed beforehand; lambda 0.1 and 0.3 show +1 point on test, inside the seed range; the three seeds share one training pool; the target-word hold-out is strict and the training set is only 289 edits; the limit may be the data and the map (linear) and not the loss. A hypothesis, untested: a map only transforms the edit it is handed, while D6 changes the edit itself (the dials) for each sentence, so no map trained on small-tuned edits may reach D6's level.

### 4.9 S2: is D6's rewordings lift really larger than a random word's? (significance test; `packs/.../s2_d6_rewordings_test.json`; log `packs/.../logs/s2_d6_rewordings_test.log`)
Why: section 4.2 said the real target's rewordings lift (24 points) is four times the random-word edit's (6 points) and that this shows the tuned change is more than a push. The 6 points came from comparing the random-word edit's 42% with the REAL target's unchanged 36%; a random word has its own unchanged level. The plan (step S2) fixed the fair test before it was run: per record, the lift of each arm against its own unchanged level, one-sided paired Wilcoxon, line p < 0.01.

| 88 primary records | Unchanged medium | With D6's dials | Lift |
|---|---|---|---|
| real target: new answer beats the true one on the rewordings | 35.8% | 60.2% | 24.4 points |
| random word: its own word beats the true one on the rewordings | 25.0% | 42.0% | 17.0 points |

Difference of the lifts +7.4 points (bootstrap 95% interval +0.0 to +15.3); records with the real lift larger, equal, smaller: 22, 54, 12; Wilcoxon p = 0.031. On all 150 test records: 19.7 and 15.7 points, difference +4.0 (interval -2.0 to +10.3), p = 0.11. Reading fixed in advance: **NOT SHOWN at this sample size.** So the rewordings contrast does not, at this sample size, show that D6's change is more than a push toward whatever word was targeted: the real lift is about 1.4 times the random word's, not 4 times, and the interval touches zero. This is a correction to a statement of this entry (4.2); the verdict of Gate 5 is not affected (its condition (b) uses the real lift only). The same unfair baseline may affect the random-word rewordings figures quoted for D7 and later steps (43% in section 4.3): the saved judge files hold no unchanged level for the random word, so this was not recomputed; to do before submission.

### 4.10 S3: the same correction for the D7 map (`packs/.../s3_random_word_baseline.json`; log `packs/.../logs/s3_random_word_baseline.log`)
Why: section 4.9 showed that a random word has its own unchanged level; the D7 table (4.3) quotes the random-word edit's rewordings (43%) next to the real target's (56%) and called it specificity to the target. The plan (step S3) fixed the recomputation before it was run: the D7 map (dose 2.0) applied through the saved judge items, each arm against its own unchanged level, same one-sided paired Wilcoxon and p < 0.01 line. Sanity checks passed (real-arm top-1 18.2%, rewordings 55.7%, neighbours 59.8%; random word 43.2% and 65.0%; the random word's unchanged rewordings level 25.0%, as in S2).

| 88 primary records, D7 map | Top-1 | Rewordings: unchanged -> with the map (lift) | Neighbours kept: unchanged -> with the map (fall) |
|---|---|---|---|
| real target | 18.2% | 35.8% -> 55.7% (19.9 points) | 67.0% -> 59.8% (7.3 points) |
| random word (its own level) | 5.7% | 25.0% -> 43.2% (18.2 points) | 77.3% -> 65.0% (12.3 points) |

Difference of the rewordings lifts +1.7 points (bootstrap 95% interval -6.8 to +10.2); records with the real lift larger, equal, smaller: 21, 49, 18; Wilcoxon p = 0.36 (150 records: 13.7 and 14.7 points, difference -1.0, p = 0.65). Reading fixed in advance: **NOT SHOWN, and not close.** For the map trained on edits, rewordings of a sentence move toward the targeted word by the same amount whether the word is the real counterfactual target or a random word: what carries to rewordings is a push toward the edit's target word, not something specific to the fact. The statement in 4.3 that the map "stays specific ... to the target (random-word edit 6%, rewordings 43% against 56%)" is withdrawn as far as rewordings go (the 13-point gap was the difference of the starting levels, 25% against 36%). Specificity that remains: on the tuned prompt (top-1 18.2% against 5.7%, though the random word also starts at a lower rank) and to the record (another record's recipe gives top-1 0% and rewordings at the unchanged level). Gate 6 and its verdict do not change (its condition (b) uses the real target's lift only), but condition (b) measures a push-like generalisation, which this result makes weaker evidence than it looked. The D10, D13 and D14 tools print the same uncorrected random-word figures; `27_random_word_baseline.py --maps ...` recomputes them for any saved map (not done).

## 6. Mistakes and corrections
- The rerun bar was written into the plan by Claude as a proposal before the author had answered; it is marked "proposed" everywhere and was never used as a gate.
- Gate 5's neighbour line (5 points) turned out stricter than the source edit's own collateral (9 points inside small). The gate was not moved; the verdict stands as FAIL and the later gate (6) uses the source's own fall, with the reason written next to it.
- In D5 the random-vector control uses a different random seed from D3, so its numbers differ from D3's in noise; the translated, wrong-recipe and norm-matched numbers were checked against the older tool on 5 records and match exactly, and a zero dose reproduces the unchanged model.
- Planning error in D7: I expected about 800 training records and about 460 successful edits. The held-out rule dropped 829 of 1,450 candidates (CounterFact reuses target words a lot), leaving 621 records and 289 successful edits. Not a change to the gate, but the training set was smaller than planned and may limit the result.
- The dose rule chosen for D7 (dev top-1 first, then dev gain, on 28 records) was fragile: in D9 it picked a bad dose in all three seeds and made longer training look 17 points worse. Found by D9b; a smoother rule (dev gain) is used from D10 on (section 4.6). I did not warn about this fragility before D9.
- In D8 the median-rank column used the lower middle value for an even number of records while the earlier tools use the upper one (the ridge reference showed 12 against 14 in D3). Found when reading the result; top-1 and the gain, which the rule uses, are unaffected; the code is fixed, the saved D8 file is not regenerated.
- A review by another model (pasted by the author) warned of vanishing gradients through the SAE's non-linearity. That does not apply here: the SAE activations are computed once and held fixed, and the change is linear in the multipliers; D6 reached rank 1 at training time on 95%. The same review called the design novel; that claim is unchecked and the closest paper is in section 8.

## 7. What this does and does not show
Shows (within this model pair, CounterFact relations, 88 records, one injection layer in D6):
- Adding the same translated change at several layers does not improve the export; the layer is not the bottleneck.
- The channel itself (small's SAE features at layer 8, a linear map, medium layer 16) can carry an edit that makes the target medium's top answer on 95% of records, when the multipliers are tuned against medium's output. The original small-tuned edit reaches 8%. The mismatch is therefore between what moves small and what moves medium, not a closed channel.
- The tuned edit carries to rewordings (60%, against 42% for the same tuning toward a random word) at a neighbour cost (8 points) close to the source edit's own (9).
- A linear map trained on edits (D7), with nothing tuned on medium at test time, roughly doubles the top-1 rate over the ridge map (8% to 18%) and halves the median rank (14 to 7), specific to the record, and on the tuned prompt to the target (its rewordings gain, however, is the same for a random word, section 4.10); it fails Gate 6.
- Longer training (40 epochs) and the amount of training data do not move a single linear map beyond about 20% top-1 (D8 hints at a rise with more data, noisy); with a sensible dose 40 epochs equal 15 epochs (D9b).
- A direct rank-1 instruction in the training loss does not help either (D10): the map fits the 289 training targets (shortfall from rank 1 about 2.4 logits to 0.15) but top-1 on unseen target words stays at 16 to 21%.
Does not show:
- A clean transfer: D6 uses medium's output for every sentence; it is a ceiling. A trained linear map (D7) gets 18%, so the gap between tuning per sentence and one trained map is large.
- That a fact moved: the channel carries a push toward any word too (random word, 83% top-1); the rewordings contrast is the only evidence of more than a push and has no significance test yet; Phase C of Entry 32 leaned toward read-out steering for the source edit itself.
- Anything about the additive edit, the Import direction, other model pairs, other relations, or generated text.

## 8. Related work (see `docs/cross_model_transfer/RELATED_WORK.md`)
Chen, Merullo, Stolfo and Pavlick (NeurIPS 2025), *Transferring Linear Features Across Language Models With Model Stitching* (arXiv 2506.06609), fit affine maps between residual streams of two models (including GPT-2 small and medium) and transfer SAEs, probes and steering vectors small to large; as summarised they report no experiment on fact edits. Our maps, stitching checks and country swap are in that family and are not claimed as new. Our different goal is carrying the change an edit makes, in both directions, with controls. The note was written from a summary of the paper, not from reading the full text; the claims must be checked before citation.
