## 1. Layman's Explanation (The Distributed Representation Map)

Following the rejection of Experiment H10 (which proved that increasing the strength of the single top driving feature is not enough to suppress competitors), we formulated the **Multi-Feature Representation Hypothesis (H11)**. 

The core idea is that the network does not represent a competitor token via a single dominant SAE feature, but rather distributes its causal influence across multiple features. If this is true, then muting only the first feature leaves several other equally strong features active, allowing the competitor token to remain dominant.

In **Experiment H11**, we measured the decay profile (how fast feature influence drops off as we move down the top 10 features) and mapped how much these features are shared (overlapped) among different competitor tokens.

## 2. Methodology & Mathematical Verification

### Verification of Delta Additivity
* **Finding**: The probability drop value returned by `get_top_competitor_features()` is defined as:
  $$\text{prob\_delta} = P_{\text{ablated}}(x) - P_{\text{clean}}(x)$$
* **Mathematical Property**: Because probability is calculated via the non-linear `softmax` normalization over the entire vocabulary logits, probability differences are **not additive**.
* **Attribution Choice**: Summing probability drops to calculate cumulative percentage attributions is mathematically invalid.
* **Alternative Metric**: To measure concentration without assuming additivity, we calculate the **decay profile** by dividing the absolute probability drop of the $k$-th ranked feature by that of the Top-1 feature:
  $$\text{Decay Ratio}_k = \frac{|\text{prob\_delta}_k|}{|\text{prob\_delta}_1|}$$

## 4. Scientific Conclusions

### Research Question Answered
> **How concentrated is a competitor's representation across SAE features?**

### Observations
1. **Flatter Decay Profiles for Semantic Competitors**:
   - Semantic competitors (e.g., `" question"`, `" doubt"`) exhibited much flatter Top-10 decay profiles than grammatical competitors. The Top-2 feature produced a probability drop that was 94.7% (for `" question"`) and 92.3% (for `" doubt"`) of the magnitude of the Top-1 feature's drop.
2. **Steeper Decay Profiles for Grammatical Competitors**:
   - Grammatical competitors (e.g., `" the"`, `" a"`, `" an"`) exhibited steep decay profiles, with subsequent features decaying quickly (dropping to ~33% - 40% of the Top-1 feature's drop).
3. **Shared Top Features**:
   - Within the discovered Top-10 influential features, every identified feature was shared by multiple competitors. Across the 18 unique features analyzed, all 18 were associated with more than one competitor.

### Interpretations
1. **Implications of Flat Decay Profiles**:
   - The relatively flat decay profiles suggest that suppressing only the highest-ranked feature may leave several similarly influential features unaffected. This pattern supports the hypothesis that multiple influential SAE features contribute to these competitors, which may reduce the effectiveness of single-feature interventions.
2. **Implications of Steep Decay Profiles**:
   - The concentration of grammatical competitors on a single shared feature indicates that Feature 313 may play a prominent role in the representation of stopword competitors.
3. **Implications of Feature Overlap**:
   - The observation that all 18 analyzed features are shared by multiple competitors suggests that competitor tokens rely on overlapping influential SAE features rather than entirely distinct feature sets.
