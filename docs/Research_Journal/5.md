## 1. Layman's Explanation (The Rank Regression Mystery)

When testing **Hybrid Mute & Boost** interventions (which simultaneously suppress competitor features and amplify target helper features), we observed a confusing phenomenon:
* At medium-sized combinations (e.g. `Mute 3 | Boost 3`), the correct target token `" Cambridge"` successfully jumped all the way to **Rank #1** as expected.
* However, when we increased the intervention size further (e.g. `Mute 3 | Boost 4`), the target token's rank **regressed** (dropped back to Rank #2), even though the target was receiving *more* boost vectors and its absolute activation was technically rising.

We launched a systematic diagnostic sweep to isolate the exact cause of this regression.

## 3. Key Findings & Scientific Conclusions

### Finding A: Implementation Integrity Validated
To confirm whether the drop in rank was caused by an implementation bug (such as state leaks or accumulated hooks carrying over from prior iterations in the sweep), we executed `Mute 3 | Boost 4` in a completely clean, isolated forward pass. 

* **Result**: The independent run produced the exact same probability (`6.735700%`) and rank (`2`).
* **Conclusion**: The hook manager correctly resets the model state between rounds. The regression is not an engineering bug, but a true mathematical feature of the model's representations.

### Finding B: Softmax Rank vs. Probability Interaction
The regression is a direct mathematical consequence of softmax competition and feature polysemanticity:

1. **Polysemantic Boosting Side-Effects**:
   - In `Mute 3 | Boost 3`, the target is Rank 1 (`7.33%`) and `" the"` is Rank 3 (`6.78%`).
   - In `Mute 3 | Boost 4`, we added the 4th target-boosting candidate (Feature 24181). Instead of helping, `" the"` rose to `7.04%` while `" Cambridge"` dropped to `6.74%`.
   - Feature 24181 is polysemantic. When boosted, it amplifies the competitor `" the"` more than it helps the target `" Cambridge"`. Due to the softmax partition function (denominator), the increased activation of `" the"` suppresses the relative probability of `" Cambridge"`, dragging its probability down and causing its rank to drop.

2. **Rank Improvement with Absolute Probability Decrease**:
   - In `Mute 2 | Boost 3` (Prob = 4.28%, Rank = 4) vs. `Mute 5 | Boost 3` (Prob = 3.80%, Rank = 3):
     - The target's absolute probability decreased, but its rank improved from 4 to 3.
     - In `Mute 2`, the competitor `" question"` sat at `6.14%` (above target). In `Mute 5`, the additional ablated features suppressed `" question"`, dropping it to `2.30%` (below target), raising the target's relative rank.
