# Research Journal Entry 36: Export, step D13: a map trained on many more, and more varied, edits

**Date:** October 11, 2026
**Branch:** `cross-model-transfer`
**Status:** Step D13 is done and **fails the pre-set Gate 9**. Adding 1,221 edits toward 759 random target words to the 289 real-target edits did not raise the map's top-1 on unseen test records (20% with the real edits only, **14%** with all of them). The map stopped memorising its training targets (training minus test top-1: 12 points down to 3) but the unseen records got worse, so "too little variety" was not what limits the map trained on edits.
**Builds on:** Entry 33 (D7 to D10, D6b). **Plan:** `docs/cross_model_transfer/PLAN.md` (step D13, Gate 9, readings written and committed before the build). **Data pack:** `docs/Research_Journal/packs/cross_model_transfer/d13_training.json`, `d13_edits_summary.json`; logs `logs/d13_varied_edits.log`, `logs/d13_varied_training.log`. **Figure:** `docs/Research_Journal/images/e36_fig1_varied_edits.png|svg`.

## 1. Why
D7 to D10 left the map trained on edits at about 20% top-1 on unseen test records. The diagnosis from D10 was memorisation: 95% top-1 on its 289 training prompts against 16% on unseen records, and the 289 training edits cover only 116 distinct target words. D6b added that D6's solution is only partly in the direction of the original edit. The natural test was to give the map many more edits with widely varied target words.

## 2. Method (`tools/transfer/21_varied_edits.py`, `22_varied_training.py`; new code only)
- **Edit generation.** The edits are made exactly as before (the project's gradient-descent multiplier edit in small, 200 candidate features at layer 8, 100 steps, only edits that reach rank 1 in small kept). Sentences: the D7 pool minus any sentence whose subject belongs to a dev or test record. Targets: two random single-token words per sentence, matched to the rank of the sentence's own counterfactual target (0.7 to 1.4 times, widened if none), never a true or counterfactual word of any dev or test record (198 banned). 2,894 attempts, 3 worker processes, 3,542 s; **1,221 reached rank 1 in small (42%)** on 749 sentences toward **759 distinct words** (the 289 real edits: 116 words). The synthetic edits are easier than the real ones: median start rank 54 against 116 (real training) and 105 (test records); success falls steeply with start rank (93% below rank 50, 6% beyond 1,000).
- **Training.** The D7 recipe: linear map small 8 to medium 16 started at the ridge map, output-matching KL loss (goal = medium's clean log-probabilities plus small's shift under the edit), Adam 2e-4, batch 32, penalty 0.1 toward the ridge map, training dose 1.0, 15 epochs, 5 texts per edit (tuned prompt, 2 rewordings, 2 neighbours). Training sets: the 289 real edits only (replicates D7 to D10), plus 25%, 50% or 100% of the synthetic edits (random subsets by seed), plus one **exploratory** set (real + the 457 synthetic edits whose target starts at rank 100 or worse in small, added after seeing that the synthetic edits are easier; not the primary result). 3 seeds each. Epoch and dose chosen on the 28 dev records by dev mean log-rank gain (the D10 protocol); judged on the same 88 primary and 150 all test records, same small-tuned recipes, same controls.
- **Gate 9 (Gate 7 unchanged), full set, mean of 3 seeds:** (a) top-1 at least 50%; (b) rewordings lift at least 80% of the edit's own lift in small; (c) neighbours fall no more than the edit itself (9.5 points); (d) real target at least 10 points above the random-word edit's top-1. Readings fixed in advance (plan): all four hold = variety solves it; (a) fails but top-1 rises at least 5 points and the training-test gap shrinks = variety helps, next a regularised map; (a) fails and top-1 stays within 5 points of the baseline = variety was not the limit.

## 4. What this shows and does not show
Shows (this map class, this model pair, these edits):
- More edits and more target words do not raise the top-1 of a single linear map on unseen real counterfactual edits; beyond 25% extra edits it falls (20%, 20%, 15%, 14%).
- The map stops memorising (gap 12 to 3 points) but the unseen records get no better, so memorisation was not the bottleneck.
- The map's behaviour on unrelated prompts becomes gentler (unrelated KL 0.035 to 0.020, top-1 flips 10% to 5%) and rewordings and rank 1 become weaker (55% to 52%, 20% to 14%): a single map trained on many different edits settles on a smaller, more average push.
- The synthetic edits being easier than the real ones (median start rank 54 against 116) is not the explanation: the exploratory set of only hard ones (457 edits) is no better (13%).
Does not show: that no map can do better (a regularised, nonlinear or edit-dependent map is untested); anything about a different loss; why the top-1 on the training prompts falls with variety while the loss does not (see section 5).

## 5. Why it failed: evidence and one correction
- The training loss is not the limit: the KL reaches the same value (0.082 against 0.085) on the real-only and the full set. What falls with variety is the share of tuned training prompts on which the target is rank 1 (32% to 18%) and, with it, the test top-1. The loss asks medium to copy small's shift in answers, not to reach rank 1; D10 showed that adding a rank-1 term makes a map fit 289 training targets without helping unseen ones. With 1,510 edits toward varied words a map with this loss reproduces the average shift and the target reaches rank 1 less often.
- **Correction of what I told the author while reading the result:** I said the map "cannot even fit the bigger set (18% on its own training prompts)". That overstates it: the loss is as low as before; what is lower is the top-1 on the training prompts, which this loss does not target. The statement in the table (training top-1 falls) stands; the explanation is that rank 1 is not what the loss asks for.
- D6b (D6's solution only partly in the original edit's direction) remains the most likely reason the limit sits on the edit side: a map transforms whatever edit it is given, while D6 changes the edit.

## 6. Mistakes and corrections
- **Time estimate.** The plan said about an hour; the run took **22,220 s (6.2 hours)**. The 100% runs took 5,200 to 5,800 s each instead of about 1,000 s: the padded training set (every text padded to 57 tokens, 7,550 texts: dS 1.3 GB, hm 1.8 GB, goal 0.8 GB) plus the models filled the 6.1 GB card (5.8 GB in use) and Windows spilled to system memory. The 25% and 50% runs had scaled well (213 to 274 s, then about 600 s). Fix for later tools: keep the training tensors on the CPU and move each batch across (numerically identical), or store unpadded.
- Two CPU rehearsals of other tools during the run slowed it a little (the 25% runs: 166 to 274 s).
- The exploratory arm was added after seeing the synthetic edits' start ranks and is labelled as such; it cannot change the verdict.
- The reading wording issue in section 3.
