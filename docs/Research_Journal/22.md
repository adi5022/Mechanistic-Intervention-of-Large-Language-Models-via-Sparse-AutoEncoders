# Research Journal Entry 22: Do Later Layers Undo the Edit? Is the SAE the Limit? (Repair and SAE-Limit Diagnostics)

**Date:** September 30, 2026
**Branch:** `pool-refill-implementation`
**Focus:** Test three worries about the layer-8 SAE edit: (1) later layers may rebuild the ablated fact ("cross-layer superposition" or self-repair), (2) the SAE may be far more machinery than a factual edit needs, (3) SAE reconstruction error may leak downstream. (Entry 21, the safety-filter study write-up, is still unwritten; see `research/paper/draft_v1/HANDOFF.md`.)

---

## 1. Summary (read this first)

> **Scope warning (added 2026-10-01).** This study used only the 3 prompts that had already failed in Entry 20 (plus MIT at layer 8), so every count here (for example "1 of 21" or "21 of 21") is a count of *layer/prompt combinations on hand-picked failures*. It is not a success rate and must not be compared with the 41/131 or 29/45 figures from the safety-filter studies. Those studies tested the safety filter, not SAE versus other methods. This diagnostic also used the default sweep settings (Top-N 120), not the project's Top-N 200 setup. The SAE-free edit is a diagnostic yardstick, not a competing method, and no fair SAE-versus-baseline comparison has been run yet (see Next steps).

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is the edit undone by later layers? | **No sign of it in these tests.** Re-adding whatever fraction of the push was lost at each later block ("held edit") gave the **same final rank as the plain edit in 21 of 21 layer/prompt cells**. The pushed direction was still 0.79 to 1.29 of its injected size at the output. |
| Is the SAE edit the limiting factor? | **Suggestive, not established.** On these 3 hand-picked failed prompts, a gradient-searched residual edit of the same L2 size reached rank 1 in 21 of 21 layer/prompt combinations and the SAE edit in 1 of 21. This says the failures are not caused by the layer or by later-layer repair. It does not say the SAE method is worse in general: the prompts were chosen because the SAE method failed on them, and no baseline has been run on the 131-prompt set. |
| How much push does the SAE-free edit need? | About **5 to 10%** of the residual norm on all tokens; **5 to 20%** on the last token alone (up to 40% at a few layers for the door prompt). The SAE edits pushed **8 to 28%** of the norm, used up to 2,294 features, and still stalled. |
| Does reconstruction error explain it? | **Not by itself.** The edit applies only the SAE delta, so the error term is never injected (Entries 1, 12, 18). It is 12 to 26% of the activation norm and is left **unedited**, so information in it is unreachable by feature scaling. |
| Is a multi-layer intervention worth building to fight repair? | **Not for that reason.** Repair was not observed. The shallower layer 7 was the only SAE success, which is a depth effect, not a repair effect. |
| What is *not* shown? | The SAE-free edit is an upper bound and may be adversarial; its side effects were **not measured**. Persistence tests the pushed *direction*, not whether a later block regenerates the fact along a *different* direction. 3 failing prompts plus one MIT control, GPT-2 small only. |

## 2. Methods (all in `src/repair_diagnostics.py`)

Prompts: the three that failed in every mode in Entry 20 (Colosseum to Rome, door to cat, "The doctor said that" to she) plus MIT to Cambridge as a control (layer 8 only). Layers 5 to 11 for the three failing prompts (layer 4 was planned but not run).

For each prompt and layer:

1. **SAE edit.** Run the normal Hybrid sweep (all positions, mute 0.6, boost 0.5, Top-N 120, strict filter, pool refill; compact records, no collateral, no combination check for speed). Take the best-so-far scale map and convert it to a residual-space delta `d = decode(scaled) - decode(original)`.
2. **Persistence trace.** Inject `d` at `blocks.L.hook_resid_pre`, cache the residual at every later block, and compute `<diff, d> / <d, d>` (1.0 = untouched, below 1 = cancelled, above 1 = amplified). Also a logit-lens rank and margin at every block, clean vs edited.
3. **Held edit (repair control).** At each later block add back `max(1 - persistence, 0) * d`, so the edit stays at full strength to the output. If this beat the plain edit, later blocks were undoing part of it.
4. **SAE-free bound.** Adam on a free residual delta at the same layer, projected to a norm ball, maximising the target log-probability (60 steps at layer 8, 40 elsewhere). Run with the norm equal to the SAE edit's (`matched`), and on a scan of 2, 5, 10, 20 and 40% of the residual norm, for last-token-only and all-non-BOS positions.
5. Also recorded: SAE reconstruction error relative to the activation norm, and the clean logit-lens margin at every block.

## 4. Interpretation

1. **Repair (#1) is not supported here.** Holding the edit to full strength changed no final rank in any cell, and persistence was near or above 1 in blocks 8 to 11. This weakens the case for building a multi-layer intervention *in order to* fight repair. Caveat: this tests the pushed direction. A later block that re-derives the fact from other directions would not show up as persistence loss and is not ruled out.
2. **On these failed prompts the layer is not the limit.** With the same push size a free residual edit works at every layer tried, while the SAE edit mostly fails. Because the prompts were selected as SAE failures, this is expected and is not evidence about the SAE method's overall success rate. The active-feature ceiling from Entry 20 and the untouched reconstruction error (12 to 26% of the activation) are consistent explanations, not proven ones.
3. **Depth still matters a little.** Layer 7 was the only SAE success and the door prompt was best at layer 8 (rank 34). Layer 11 was worst for Colosseum (rank 6). With one success in 21 cells, no layer ranking is claimed.
4. **The Lamborghini point is fair but incomplete.** The edit itself is a sum of decoder directions, and a plain residual edit does better here. But the SAE also supplies feature-level selection, the safety filter and interpretability, none of which the SAE-free edit has. The missing piece is the side-effect comparison.

## 5. Limitations

- The SAE-free edit maximises target probability freely (its final probabilities are near 1.0), so it is a bound and probably a damaging edit. No collateral (KL on neutral prompts) was measured for it.
- The SAE-side rank is the best-so-far of a sweep, not necessarily the final step.
- Optimiser steps were 60 at layer 8 and 40 at the other layers; sensitivity to the step count was not tested.
- One base model (GPT-2 small), one SAE release, four prompts, single runs (no seeds; the optimiser starts from zero so it is deterministic on a given GPU).
- Layer 4 was planned and not run. Timing: about 2.5 to 3 minutes per prompt-layer on a shared GTX 1660 Ti.

## 7. Next steps

1. Measure collateral damage (KL on the 20 neutral prompts) for the SAE-free edit, to see whether it is a usable baseline or just an adversarial vector.
2. Add a simple non-SAE baseline (contrastive steering vector or ROME-style edit at layers 7 to 8) on the 131-prompt set.
3. Test whether the SAE-free direction lies outside the span of the SAE decoder (does the ceiling come from the error term?).
4. Only if 1 to 3 change the picture, revisit multi-layer editing.
