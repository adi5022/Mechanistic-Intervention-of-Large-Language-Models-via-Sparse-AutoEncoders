\chapter*{\centering Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Language models sometimes rank a correct completion below competing tokens. Fixing this by changing weights is permanent and degrades as edits accumulate; fixing it by adding text to the prompt does not use what the model already computes. This project, \textbf{FeatureScalpel}, studies a \textbf{transient, weight-free alternative}: during one forward pass the residual stream of GPT-2 Small is edited through the features of a pretrained \textbf{sparse autoencoder (SAE)}, and the edit disappears when the pass ends. Per-feature gradient descent on the multipliers of 200 candidate features brought the true answer to rank 1 on 271 of 300 held-out CounterFact prompts, against 161 for a sweep given the same range and 94 for the original sweep settings. Two limits of that work motivated the second part of the project: every edit needs an SAE for its own model, and reaching rank 1 by steering proved weak evidence, since an additive edit forced random unrelated words to rank 1 on 20 of 20 prompts.

The second part asks whether the change an edit makes inside one model can be carried into another at inference time, with no weight change. A linear map between the residual streams of GPT-2 Small and GPT-2 Medium keeps the receiving model working (per-dimension $R^2$ 0.58 and 0.66) and carries a known meaning change far above controls (55\% and 33\%). An edit tuned only in Small, translated and added to Medium (\emph{Export}), moves the target's median rank from 142 to 14 but makes it the top answer on only 8\% of 88 test records. Seven further attempts at a better translator, injection layer, training signal or amount of training data stopped at about 20\% at best, whereas tuning the edit against Medium's output for each sentence reached 95\%, a ceiling and not a transfer. The effect on reworded prompts is the same for a random target word as for the true one, so what carries is a push toward the targeted word, not a fact. In the other direction (\emph{Import}), replacing Small's state by Medium's translated state rescues one in three of the facts Small gets wrong but lowers its overall accuracy, whereas a half-and-half mix raises accuracy on held-out facts from 8.5\% to 9.5\% (Medium alone: 10.2\%). Small's SAE directions raise the steering success from 81\% to 95\% over random directions but are not required for it.

All pass bars were fixed before the runs, every result is stored with its logs, and the limits are stated: one model pair, one dataset, 88 test records, no generated-text or other-model tests.
