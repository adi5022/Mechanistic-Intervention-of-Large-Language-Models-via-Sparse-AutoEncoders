\chapter{Discussion, Conclusion and Future Work}\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{What the Work Shows}

The sweep was held back by the supply of features, not by the selection rule. Taking candidates from all prompt positions made the pool about five times larger and raised the prompts at rank 1 from 4 to 10 of 13. Choosing the right features and strengths mattered more than stronger interventions. Muting the competitor's drivers and adding boosts gave the early gains. One better global strength pair lifted a stand-in success rate from 25\% to 39\%, and choosing strengths per prompt added nothing we could measure. Tuning one multiplier per feature on the prompt itself reached 271 of 300 on the real model.

Rank 1 turned out to be a weak test. The additive edit forced random words to rank 1 on 20 of 20 prompts, and an optimiser with enough freedom can reach any output you pick. Being able to steer a target to the top says nothing about what the model knows, in either direction. The edit itself is a push toward the answer's output direction. It carries little beyond the prompt it was tuned on, and it mostly ignores an instruction not to say the word. The side study showed that a linear channel between GPT-2 Small and Medium keeps the receiving model working, that an exported edit moves the target in a specific way but rarely all the way to rank 1, that what crosses is a push and not a fact, and that mixing in half of Medium's state raises Small's accuracy by one point.

Four habits paid off: controls first, fair comparisons, pass bars fixed before each run, and corrections made in the open. Withdrawn claims are marked in the journal where they happened.

\section{Threats to Validity and What Is Not Shown}

\begin{itemize}
  \item We use one model family, one older SAE release, and layer 8 in all later studies. The early studies use small, templated prompt sets (13 to 131 prompts), and like CounterFact they treat prompts as independent, without correction for multiple comparisons.
  \item Success means rank 1 on the next token, and the median target probability at rank 1 is only 7.7\% for gradient descent. We measured side effects on the same prompt, on 20 unrelated prompts and in 40-prompt tests, with one seed, no confidence intervals, and true targets only in the generation and instruction tests.
  \item The headroom and the learned strengths are stand-ins measured on cached states, and we never ran the real sweep with the learned strengths. The side study has 88 primary test records.
  \item \textbf{We have not compared the approach with ROME, MEMIT, in-context editing, prompting or difference-of-means steering}, so nothing here says how it ranks against them.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

FeatureScalpel set out to repair suppressed factual recall in GPT-2 Small through the features of a sparse autoencoder, without changing a single weight. After many attempts that mostly failed in useful ways, we arrived at a transient edit that brings the true answer to rank 1 on 271 of 300 held-out prompts, quickly and with small side effects on the same prompt. The applied side study found that the change an edit makes can be carried between models as a push, that importing a larger model's state gives a small gain, and that a fact does not cross. The results support the claim that a transient SAE-feature edit can raise a suppressed answer to the top, and that tuning a multiplier per feature does so more often, faster and with lower same-prompt side effects than a hand-built sweep. They do not support the claim that such edits recover knowledge, that they are specific to a fact, or that they beat weight editing, in-context editing or prompting.

\section{Future Work}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A specificity benchmark.} For every method, on the same prompts, it should measure whether the target reaches rank 1, carries over to rewordings, leaves unrelated prompts and generated text alone, and respects an instruction, with random words of matched starting rank as controls.
  \item \textbf{A head-to-head comparison} with ROME, MEMIT, in-context editing and difference-of-means steering, using existing implementations \cite{r32} and timing everything on one machine.
  \item \textbf{More strengths, transfer and scale.} We would run the learned strengths in the real sweep, rerun the controls for the Import mix, and try other model pairs (for example Gemma 3 270M and 1B, which have SAEs on both sides) and larger models with pretrained SAEs \cite{r19}.
\end{enumerate}
