\chapter{Applied Study: Carrying an Edit to Another Model}\label{ch:transfer}

This chapter is a \emph{side study}. It is not the next version of the system of Chapters~\ref{ch:method} and~\ref{ch:evolution}; it applies the tools that work produced (the edit, the benchmark, the controls and the habit of fixing pass bars first) to a new question, to see whether the interpretability and intervention work has fruits beyond one model. It was done in October 2026 (journal 31 to 37).

\section{The Question and the Design}

\textit{Can the change an edit makes inside GPT-2 Small be translated and added to GPT-2 Medium at inference time, with no weight change, and can Medium's state improve Small?} We call the first direction \textbf{Export} (small to medium) and the second \textbf{Import} (medium to small); Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows both. The two models do not share internal coordinates (width 768 against 1024), so a \emph{translator} is needed, and Section~\ref{sec:additive-story} showed that ``the answer reaches rank 1'' is weak evidence, so every test carries controls.

\textbf{Models and data.} Small is GPT-2 Small with its layer-8 SAE; medium is GPT-2 Medium (24 layers, width 1024, no SAE); both share one tokenizer. Small's layer 8 is paired with medium's layer 16. The benchmark is 200 CounterFact records from the hard set of Section~\ref{sec:scale}, split into 50 development (dev) and 150 test records; dev records choose settings (layer, dose, epoch) and test records are only read. The edit tuned in small reaches rank 1 on 116 of the 200; the \textbf{88 primary test records} are those test records on which it does, since only there is there an edit to carry. Medium's median rank of the target before anything is done is 142.

\textbf{The translator.} Only the \emph{change} of an edit is translated, so the bias of an affine map cancels and the translated change is $\Delta_S W$ (zero at the first position). Four ways of obtaining $W$ were tried: (1) a \emph{ridge map} fitted on ordinary text, $W=\arg\min\lVert(H_S-\mu_S)W-(H_M-\mu_M)\rVert^2+\lambda\lVert W\rVert^2$, over paired states of the two models on wikitext-2 (500,126 fit tokens) \cite{r45}; (2) a \emph{neural map} (ridge plus a one-hidden-layer correction); (3) a \emph{linear map trained on edits}, started from the ridge map and trained so that medium's answers move as small's do, with goal $q=\mathrm{softmax}(\ell_M+\ell_S^{\mathrm{edit}}-\ell_S)$ and loss $\mathrm{KL}(q\,\Vert\,p_M(h_M+\alpha\Delta_SW))$ over 289 successful training edits; and (4) \emph{no fixed map}: the 200 multipliers re-tuned for each sentence against \emph{medium's} output, a ceiling and not a transfer, since it looks at medium every time.

\textbf{Export, Import and controls.} Export adds the translated change while medium reads a sentence, $h_M'=h_M+\alpha\,\Delta_S W$ at layer 16 at every position but the first; the \textbf{dose} $\alpha$ is chosen on dev (from step D10 on by the best mean log-rank gain, since dev top-1 on 28 records proved fragile). Import mixes small's state with medium's translated state, $h_S'=(1-\gamma)h_S+\gamma\,T(h_M)$, with $\gamma=1$ full replacement. The receiver is never shown the answer. Controls: a random vector of the same size; another record's edit; an edit toward a random word of matched starting rank; a \emph{word push} along the target's output direction (it uses the answer, so it is a comparison and not a method); the fact stated in medium's prompt; for Import, a state from a wrong sentence, the average state and a random map. \emph{Rewordings} and \emph{neighbours} are the shares of reworded prompts on which the new answer beats the true one, and of other-subject prompts on which the true answer still wins. \textbf{Every pass bar (``gate'') was written into the project plan before the run that judged it and never moved}; a step designed after seeing a result is labelled post-hoc and cannot change a verdict (Appendix~B).

\section{The Channel Works}\label{sec:channel}

The ridge map fits well enough to keep the receiver working: per-dimension $R^2$ is 0.58 (small layer 8 to medium layer 16) and 0.66 (medium 16 to small 8). Stitching one model's first layers to the other's last layers through the map recovers 91\% (Export direction) and 98\% (Import direction) of the receiver's own quality. The map copies \emph{which word changed} well (cosine 0.63 to 0.76) but the summary at the last position only weakly (0.24 to 0.31), so the translator passes its pre-set bar for Import and not for Export.

A positive control swaps one country for another in a template sentence: the difference of the source model's states is translated and added to the receiver, and a hit is counted when the receiver's top answer becomes the swapped capital. The translated change succeeds on \textbf{55\%} (Export) and \textbf{33\%} (Import) of test cases against at most 0.4\% for a random vector, a random map and another pair's difference. It is a channel check, not an edit or a fact.

\section{Export: a Real but Weak Effect}\label{sec:export}

For each of the 88 primary test records the edit tuned in small is relayed to medium through the ridge map and added at layer 16 (Table~\ref{tab:export}). The effect is real, graded and specific: the target's median rank in medium falls from 142 to \textbf{14}, it becomes the top answer on \textbf{8\%} of records, reworded prompts favour the new answer on 52\% against 36\% unchanged, and neighbours lose only 4 points. A plain word push makes the target first on 83\% of records but only 23\% of neighbours keep the true answer, and stating the fact in medium's prompt costs 23 points of neighbours. The pre-set bar (top-1 of at least 15\%, 10 points above the strongest control) was not met.

\section{Searching for a Better Translator}\label{sec:search}

Because the effect is real but weak, we tried seven changes, each with its pass rule written first (Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}; Appendix~B). \textbf{Layer and capacity:} injecting at layers 4, 8, 12, 16 and 20 gave 0, 5, 5, 8 and 1\%, and splitting the dose over several layers did no better, so the layer is not the limit; a neural map fits ordinary text much better ($R^2$ +0.09 to +0.11) but exports no better (8\%), so a better fit to text does not help. \textbf{A map trained on edits} (method 3) roughly doubled the result to 18\% top-1 (median rank 14 to 7, paired $p=5\times10^{-9}$) and stayed specific to the record, but more training edits, longer training and a direct rank-1 term in the loss left it at about 20\%. (One dose rule, dev top-1 on 28 records, gave a misleading 2\% against 20\% and was replaced, post hoc, by the dev log-rank gain.)

\textbf{Why it plateaus.} With the rank-1 term the map learned its 289 training targets (95\% top-1 on them) but not unseen words (16\%): it memorises. Giving it 1,221 more edits toward 759 random target words removed the memorisation but unseen records got \emph{worse}, 20\% to 14\%, because the loss asks medium to copy small's shift, not to reach rank 1. \textbf{Per-sentence tuning is a ceiling:} re-tuning the multipliers against medium's output reaches the top answer on 95\% of records (84\% on all 150), with the same neighbour cost as the source edit inside small (8 points against 9). The channel can carry a rank-1 edit; what fails is the compatibility of an edit tuned for small with medium. The ceiling's solution is only partly in the direction of the original edit (cosine 0.51 against 0.02 by chance), so no map that merely transforms the given edit can supply what is missing; it also reaches 83\% for a random word.

\section{What Is Carried: a Fact or a Push?}\label{sec:push}

The same question as in Section~\ref{sec:additive-story} applies to Export. Each arm's gain on reworded prompts must be measured against its \emph{own} unchanged level (a random word starts lower, at 25\% against 36\%). Doing so, the per-sentence multipliers gain 24.4 points for the real target and 17.0 for a random word (difference +7.4, 95\% interval 0.0 to +15.3, $p=0.031$), and the map trained on edits gains 19.9 and 18.2 ($p=0.36$). Neither difference passes the $p<0.01$ line fixed beforehand. An earlier ``four times'' claim compared the wrong baselines and was withdrawn. What carries to reworded prompts is a push toward the targeted word, not something specific to the fact. A further control repeated the per-sentence tuning with 200 random directions in place of the 200 SAE directions: the SAE directions reached 95\% and the random ones 81\%, a difference of 15 points, so the SAE makes the tuning more effective but is not what makes steering possible.

\section{Import: Medium's State into Small}\label{sec:import}

Of the 21,789 single-token CounterFact facts left after the development and pilot records are removed, medium alone gets 9.9\% right and small 8.3\%; 780 facts are right in medium and wrong in small. \textbf{Full replacement} of small's layer-8 state by medium's translated state rescues 262 of the 780 (33.6\%; controls at most 0.8\%), but breaks 22\% of the facts both models knew and 70\% of those only small knew, so small's accuracy falls from 8.30\% to 7.17\%. The pre-set bar (50\% rescue, accuracy half-way to medium, 90\% of known facts kept) was not met, although the controls show that real information crosses.

A \textbf{softer mix} was then tried, with its rule written before the build: $\gamma\in\{0.1,\dots,1\}$ chosen on one half of the facts and reported on the other (10,895 facts). Mixing in half of medium's state ($\gamma=0.5$) raises small's accuracy from 8.5\% to \textbf{9.5\%} (medium alone: 10.2\%), gaining 162 facts and losing 53 ($p=2.5\times10^{-14}$), keeps 98\% of the facts both models knew and rescues 28.6\% of the facts small gets wrong (Figure~\ref{fig:import}). This meets the rule (gain of at least 0.5 points, 90\% of known facts kept, $p<0.01$) and is the first net-positive Import result. It is a one-point gain, it needs medium to run on the same sentence, and the replacement test's controls were not rerun for the mix.

\section{What the Side Study Shows}

A linear channel between GPT-2 Small and Medium exists and keeps the receiver working. The change an edit makes in small moves medium's answers specifically, in a graded way and without damaging its neighbours, but every translator that does not look at medium's output stops at about 20\% top-1, far below the 50\% bar, while looking at medium for every sentence reaches 95\%. Together with the controls this puts the limit on the \emph{edit's compatibility}, not on the channel, the layer, the translator's capacity or the amount of data, and what crosses is a push toward the targeted word and not a fact. Limits: one model pair, one dataset and one SAE; 88 primary test records; the additive edit, generated text and other pairs untested; and the map follows earlier stitching work \cite{r44}.
