Language models often rank a correct answer below competing words even though the answer is within reach. Fixing this by changing weights is permanent and gets worse as edits pile up, and adding text to the prompt does not use what the model already computes. This project, \textbf{FeatureScalpel}, asks whether the fix can be \textbf{transient and weight-free}. During one forward pass the residual stream of GPT-2 Small is edited through the features of a pretrained \textbf{sparse autoencoder (SAE)}, and the edit disappears when the pass ends.

The report follows the work as it happened, mistakes included. Muting the target's own helpers corrected 1 of 22 test facts and muting the leading competitor corrected 3. Muting alone failed on our probe prompt, while muting together with boosting brought it to rank 1. Safety filtering, a study of depth, a batched implementation (186 model calls per prompt and layer down to 6), pool refill and candidates from all prompt positions followed. A small network that chose the strengths did no better per prompt than one learned pair of numbers, but tuning one multiplier per feature by gradient descent worked far better. On 300 held-out CounterFact prompts it brought the true answer to rank 1 on 271, against 161 for a sweep given the same range and 94 for the original settings, with lower side effects and 5 to 6.5 times less compute. An additive edit then pushed random, unrelated words to rank 1 on 20 of 20 prompts, so reaching rank 1 says little about what a model knows. A side study applied the same tools to a new question: can an edit be carried from GPT-2 Small to GPT-2 Medium? A linear translator keeps the receiving model working. An exported edit lifts the target's median rank from 142 to 14 but reaches rank 1 on only 8\% of records. What crosses is a push toward a word, not a fact. Mixing half of Medium's state into Small raises Small's accuracy from 8.5\% to 9.5\%. The side study's pass bars were fixed before the runs, and its limits (one model family, one dataset, no comparison with ROME or in-context editing) are stated.
