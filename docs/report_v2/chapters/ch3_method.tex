\chapter{The Edit and How It Works}\label{ch:method}

This chapter gives the mathematics of the final system as it is implemented in \texttt{src/hooks.py}, \texttt{src/editing.py}, \texttt{src/hybrid\_runner.py} and \texttt{src/gradient\_editing.py}. Chapter~\ref{ch:evolution} then shows how each piece came about.

\section{Setting and Notation}

GPT-2 Small reads a prompt and, at the last position, gives a probability for every possible next token. The \textbf{residual stream} is the running sum that every attention and MLP block adds its output to \cite{r53}. We write $x_p\in\mathbb{R}^{768}$ \cite{r1} for its value at position $p$ at the hook point \texttt{blocks.L.hook\_resid\_pre}, with layer $L=8$ unless stated otherwise. For a prompt whose true answer is $t$, the \textbf{rank} of $t$ is its place in the next-token ordering (1 is first), and the \textbf{blocker} $b$ is the token currently ranked first. An edit \textbf{succeeds} when $t$ becomes first. That is a low bar (Section~\ref{sec:additive-story}), so we always read it together with side-effect measures and controls.

\section{SAE Features and Delta Patching}

For the activation $x_p$ the SAE gives sparse feature activations and a reconstruction,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sae}
f_p=\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(W_{\mathrm{enc}}(x_p-b_{\mathrm{dec}})+b_{\mathrm{enc}}\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{24576},\qquad \hat{x}_p=W_{\mathrm{dec}}^{\top}f_p+b_{\mathrm{dec}},
\end{equation}
where row $W_{\mathrm{dec}}[k]$ is the direction of feature $k$ in the residual stream. If we simply replaced $x_p$ by $\hat{x}_p$ we would inject the SAE's reconstruction error (12 to 26\% of the activation norm) and disturb the model even with no edit. So we add only the change in the decoded output. If feature $k$ is scaled by $s_k$, with $s_k=1$ for features left alone, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta}
\Delta_p=\sum_{k}(s_k-1)\,f_{p,k}\,W_{\mathrm{dec}}[k],\qquad x'_p=x_p+\Delta_p ,
\end{equation}
at every prompt position $p$. Muting with strength $\mu\in[0,1]$ means $s_k=1-\mu$, so $\mu=1$ removes the feature, and boosting with strength $\beta\ge0$ means $s_k=1+\beta$. When every $s_k=1$ the edit is exactly zero. The formulation was fixed in journal 12 \cite{p5}.

\section{Choosing Candidates: Causal Ranking}\label{sec:selection}

Ranking all 24,576 features by removing each one would take as many forward passes. We do it in two stages instead. \textbf{Stage 1} screens. One clean pass caches the residual stream, the SAE encodes it, and we keep the top $N$ features by activation, either at the last token or over \emph{all} prompt positions except the start token, scoring each feature by its maximum activation. \textbf{Stage 2} ranks by cause. Each candidate is removed on its own and we record how a token's probability changes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:causal}
\Delta p_k(v)=P_{\mathrm{ablate}(k)}(v)-P_{\mathrm{clean}}(v).
\end{equation}
For the blocker ($v=b$) the most negative values are its strongest drivers, which form the \emph{mute list}. For the target ($v=t$) the most negative values are its strongest helpers, which form the \emph{boost list}. The $N$ removals are stacked into the batch dimension of a single model call, so ranking costs two batched calls instead of $2N+1$ separate ones \cite{p5}.

\section{The Hybrid Mute and Boost Sweep}\label{sec:sweep}

The \textbf{safety filter} tests every candidate alone at its strength and keeps it only if the target does not get worse:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:safe}
\mathrm{safe}(k)\iff r_{k}(t)\le r_{0}(t)\;\wedge\;p_{k}(t)-p_{0}(t)\ge-10^{-6},
\end{equation}
where $r$ and $p$ are the target's rank and probability with feature $k$ applied alone, and $r_0,p_0$ are the baseline values. A feature that is safe on both sides is kept on one side only. The sweep then applies the first $k$ mutes and the first $k$ boosts together in one forward pass, for $k=1,2,\dots$, and stops when the target is first. It reports the best step and not the last one, because adding features that are each safe does not always help. When both pools are used up, \textbf{pool refill} keeps every feature applied so far, makes one pass to get the \emph{steered baseline} and its new blocker, and ranks and filters the remaining features again, including ones rejected earlier \cite{p3,p6}. Figure~\ref{fig:algo} gives the whole loop.

\section{The Gradient-Descent Editor}\label{sec:gd-method}

The sweep decides which features to use and how many, with one strength per side. The gradient-descent editor instead gives each of $K=200$ candidate features its own multiplier and tunes all of them for the prompt at hand. With free numbers $z_k$, set at the start so that no feature changes, the multiplier is $m_k=1+a_k$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a}
a_k=-1+3\,\sigma(z_k),\qquad m_k\in(0,3),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the logistic function. With $A_{p,k}$ the clean activation of feature $k$ at position $p$, the edit is Equation~\ref{eq:delta},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gdedit}
\Delta_p=\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_k\,A_{p,k}\,W_{\mathrm{dec}}[k].
\end{equation}
We run blocks 8 to 11 from the edited state and read the logits $\ell$ at the last position. The loss is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
\mathcal{L}=\max\!\bigl(0,\;\gamma-(\ell_t-\max_{v\ne t}\ell_v)\bigr)+\lambda_{\mathrm{KL}}\,\mathbf{1}[\text{target leads}]\,\mathrm{KL}+\lambda_{\mathrm{size}}\sum_k|a_k|\,w_k ,
\end{equation}
with margin $\gamma=0.3$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{KL}}=3$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{size}}=0.005$, and $w_k$ the feature's share of the total maximum activation. The first term asks the target to beat every other token by $\gamma$ logits. The second penalises changes to the rest of the distribution, measured by the Kullback-Leibler divergence \cite{r54},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl}
\mathrm{KL}=\sum_{v\notin\{t,b\}}q_v\log\frac{q_v}{\tilde p_v},\qquad q=\mathrm{softmax}\bigl(\ell^{\mathrm{clean}}_{\setminus\{t,b\}}\bigr),\ \ \tilde p=\mathrm{softmax}\bigl(\ell_{\setminus\{t,b\}}\bigr),
\end{equation}
taken over all tokens except the target and the blocker, so that the intended change is not penalised. It is charged only once the target leads, which matches the sweep's stopping rule. The third term prefers small edits. Only the $z_k$ are updated, by Adam \cite{r36} (learning rate 0.1, 100 steps), and GPT-2's weights and the SAE are frozen and receive no gradient. We keep the best iterate and re-check it through the same hook as the sweep in a full forward pass, so the reported rank does not depend on the shortcut used during tuning \cite{p13}. \section{The Additive Edit}\label{sec:additive-method}

A multiplier cannot switch on a silent feature, because $0\times m=0$ (Figure~\ref{fig:addmath}). The additive extension adds an amount $c_k\ge0$ of the decoder direction of selected silent features, at the last position only:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:add}
\text{new volume}=\text{old volume}\times m+c,\qquad \Delta_{\text{last}}\mathrel{+}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{S}}c_k\,W_{\mathrm{dec}}[k].
\end{equation}
The set $\mathcal{S}$ holds the 200 features that are exactly silent at the last position and have the largest positive first-order score $W_{\mathrm{dec}}[k]\cdot g$, where $g=\partial(\ell_t-\max_{v\ne t}\ell_v)/\partial x_{\mathrm{last}}$ is the gradient of the target's margin with respect to the residual. Each amount is $c_k=\mathrm{cap}\cdot(\sigma(s_k)-\sigma(s_0))$ for $s_k\ge s_0$ and 0 otherwise. Every amount starts at exactly zero, $\mathrm{cap}$ is the loudest feature on the real prompt positions, and an extra loss $0.005\sum_kc_k/\mathrm{cap}$ discourages large additions. The score is a first-order estimate of how the margin changes. That is why this edit writes the \emph{output direction of the answer} and not a representation of a fact (Section~\ref{sec:additive-story}).

\section{Benchmark and Metrics}\label{sec:bench}

The benchmark (16,360 CounterFact prompts, a subject-safe split, 300 test prompts) is described in Section~\ref{sec:scale}. We report the \emph{rank} and \emph{probability} of the target; the \emph{same-prompt KL} (Equation~\ref{eq:kl}); the \emph{edit size}, which is the norm of the change divided by the norm of the residual at the last position; and the effect of the same edit on 20 unrelated prompts, on CounterFact's reworded and neighbouring prompts, and on generated text. The reworded and neighbour scores are ROME's paraphrase and neighbourhood scores \cite{r2} (Table~\ref{tab:metricmap}), which lets a reader compare them with the editing literature. Our success criterion, rank 1 over the whole vocabulary, is stricter than ROME's efficacy score, which compares only the new and the true answer. Methods are compared on the same prompts with the exact McNemar test \cite{r39} on reaching rank 1 and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test \cite{r40} on KL, and bootstrap intervals \cite{r57} are used where a difference of two shares is reported. We made our choices on validation prompts and only read the test prompts.
