\chapter{Literature Review}\label{ch:literature-review}

This chapter reviews the four research threads on which the project draws and states the gap it addresses.

\section{Knowledge Editing}

ROME \cite{r2} locates a factual association in the mid-layer MLPs of GPT-style models and rewrites one weight matrix in closed form; MEMIT \cite{r3} extends this to thousands of edits. Both change the weights permanently. Gupta \textit{et al.} \cite{r4} showed that sequential ROME and MEMIT edits first erase earlier edits gradually and then collapse the model abruptly, and Hase \textit{et al.} \cite{r30} found that where a fact is localised need not predict where it can be edited best. Methods that leave the weights alone add the fact as text: in-context editing \cite{r31} places the corrected fact and demonstrations in the prompt, and retrieval-augmented generation \cite{r34} conditions the model on retrieved documents. Both change the input and not the model's internal computation. EasyEdit \cite{r32} collects these methods in one framework. The CounterFact dataset of Meng \textit{et al.} \cite{r2}, which pairs a prompt with a true and a counterfactual answer, is the benchmark used throughout this report.

\section{Activation Steering and Sparse Autoencoders}

Activation steering adds a direction to the residual stream at inference time \cite{r9,r10,r11}. Superposition \cite{r5} means that single neurons rarely correspond to single concepts; sparse autoencoders \cite{r6,r7,r8} learn a wide sparse basis whose features are often interpretable, and open SAEs now exist for GPT-2 Small \cite{r15}, for Gemma \cite{r19} and others, with browsable feature explanations \cite{r20}. Several studies edit SAE features directly: Chalnev \textit{et al.} \cite{r28} target SAE features to limit side effects of steering vectors, Zhao \textit{et al.} \cite{r29} edit a few features to control whether a model relies on its memory or on the context, Farrell \textit{et al.} \cite{r27} use feature ablation to unlearn knowledge, Marks \textit{et al.} \cite{r33} use features as nodes of editable circuits, and Arad \textit{et al.} \cite{r23} show that selecting features by their effect on the output improves steering. The evidence is mixed: AxBench \cite{r12} reports that simple baselines can beat SAEs for steering, a Google DeepMind update \cite{r24} reports negative results for SAEs on downstream tasks, and Chaudhary and Geiger \cite{r25} and Cui \textit{et al.} \cite{r26} report limits when SAEs are used to disentangle or to intervene on factual knowledge. Steering that reaches a target token is also known to be weak evidence about what a model contains, which is why this project uses controls throughout.

\section{Comparing and Stitching Representations}

Model stitching connects the lower layers of one network to the upper layers of another through a small learned layer; if the stitched network still works, the two representations are compatible \cite{r41,r42}. Representational similarity measures such as CKA \cite{r43} compare activations without stitching. For language models, Chen \textit{et al.} \cite{r44} fit affine maps between the residual streams of two models, including GPT-2 Small and GPT-2 Medium, and use them to transfer SAEs, probes and steering vectors from a small model to a larger one. \todo{this description of \cite{r44} was written from the paper's summary; check it against the full text before submission} Our translator is of this family: a ridge regression \cite{r45} fitted on ordinary text. We do not claim the map as new.

\section{Research Gap}

Knowledge editing changes weights or the prompt; SAE steering works inside one model; stitching work transfers vectors learned in one model, such as SAEs, probes and steering directions. To our knowledge, based on a limited search, no work has asked whether the change that a fact edit makes inside one model can be carried into another, in both directions, with controls that separate a carried fact from a push toward a word, and with every pass bar fixed before the run. This report supplies that test for GPT-2 Small and Medium, including the negative results.
