# Experiment: Weighted Multi-Competitor Reduction & Grammatical Feature Collapse on the Cambridge Case

**Experiment ID**: EXP-004  
**Date**: 2026-07-24  
**Status**: Completed  

---

## 1. Context & Motivation
### Question
Can we successfully clear the path for a suppressed correct target token by muting all above-target competitor tokens simultaneously, scaled in proportion to their threat level (probability)?

### Background
In prior iterations of competitor-focused ablation (EXP-002), the target token `" Cambridge"` remained stuck at Rank 3. When we ablated the top competitor `" the"`, the secondary competitor `" a"` immediately rose to take its place. Single-competitor interventions failed to move the rank when multiple competitors were clustered closely.

### Motivation
We need a joint intervention methodology that targets all above-target competitors in a single forward pass, while ensuring that the overall combination does not introduce new blockers or regress the target rank.

---

## 2. Hypothesis & Setup
### Hypotheses
* **H4**: Suppressing all competitor tokens ranked above the target simultaneously, using threat-proportional weights, will allow the correct target token to rise safely in rank.
* **H5**: Highly common grammatical competitors (such as `" the"`, `" a"`, `" an"`) are represented in the residual stream by generic syntactic features rather than clean semantic tokens. Consequently, their representations are highly distributed, leading to feature collapse when mapping tokens to single features.

### Experimental Setup
* **Model**: GPT-2-small (Layer 8 Residual Stream, `blocks.8.hook_resid_pre`)
* **SAE**: `gpt2-small-res-jb`
* **Test Case**: 
  - **Prompt**: `"The location of Massachusetts Institute of Technology is in"`
  - **Target**: `" Cambridge"` (Clean Rank: 8, Clean Prob: 1.15%)
  - **Top Competitors**: `" the"` (17.95%), `" a"` (10.53%), `" question"` (7.51%), `" jeopardy"` (2.71%), `" an"` (2.63%), `" danger"` (1.76%), `" doubt"` (1.21%).

---

## 3. Results & Observations
### Results
When running the diagnostic trace of competitor-to-feature mappings, we recorded the following raw mappings:

* `' the'` (17.95%) → **Feature 313**
* `' a'` (10.53%) → **Feature 313**
* `' an'` (2.63%) → **Feature 313**
* `' question'` (7.51%) → **Feature 21169**
* `' jeopardy'` (2.71%) → **Feature 21169**
* `' danger'` (1.76%) → **Feature 21169**
* `' doubt'` (1.21%) → **Feature 21169**

The 7 competitors collapsed completely onto **2 unique features** (313 and 21169). Using the max-of-shared-strength rule, the resulting intervention was:
* **Feature 313**: Muted at strength `0.284` (driven by `' the'`)
* **Feature 21169**: Muted at strength `0.119` (driven by `' question'`)

Post-intervention results:
- **Target New Rank**: 8 (unchanged)
- **Target New Prob**: 1.29% (shifted minimally from 1.15%)
- **Safety Status**: Safe (no new blockers, target rank did not regress, but did not improve either).

### Observations
Muting the single top feature for each competitor was not enough to lower their probabilities because the residual support from other active features (representing other reasons for predicting `" the"` or `" a"`) remained untouched.

### Interpretation
* **H4 is partially rejected**: Suppressing only the *top representative feature* of each competitor is insufficient when the competitors are generic grammatical tokens.
* **H5 is validated**: The grammatical tokens exhibited clear collapse onto Feature 313. This supports the **Distributed Support (Loudspeaker) Hypothesis**: common syntax-driven tokens are backed by many independent features in the network, so muting only the single loudest driver does not silence the prediction.

---

## 4. Discussion & Next Steps
### Limitations
* The single-feature representation assumption (`1 competitor -> 1 feature`) is a bottleneck for grammatical tokens.
* Factual tokens (e.g. `" Rome"`, `" Paris"`) are monosemantic and easy to ablate, whereas syntactic tokens are polysemantic and highly distributed.

### Next Steps
* Transition from muting a single representative feature per competitor to identifying and muting a **subspace** of several/all influential driving features for each competitor.
* Test weighted reduction on factual competitor clusters (where competitors are specific facts, e.g. competing country names, rather than stopwords).
