## 1. Context & Objective
### Question
How concentrated is a competitor's representation across SAE features? Does the strongest SAE feature explain most of a competitor's behavior, or is support distributed across multiple influential features?

### Background
Weighted Multi-Competitor Reduction (EXP-008) failed to suppress competitor tokens on the Cambridge MIT benchmark even when using additive aggregation with high mute strengths. This suggests that the single-feature competitor assumption (`1 competitor -> 1 feature`) is a core bottleneck. 

This experiment performs a diagnostic-only measurement to map the exact distribution of competitor representations.

---

## 2. Methodology & Verification of Delta Additivity
Before performing cumulative analysis, we verified the mathematical definition of `delta` returned by `get_top_competitor_features()`:
$$\text{prob\_delta} = P_{\text{ablated}}(x) - P_{\text{clean}}(x)$$

### Verification Result
* **Non-Additivity**: Probability differences are **not** additive. This is because probability is computed via the non-linear `softmax` normalization over the entire vocab logits. 
* **Implication**: Summing probability deltas to express cumulative percentage attributions is mathematically invalid.
* **Alternative Metric**: To measure concentration without assuming additivity, we calculate the **decay profile** by dividing the absolute probability drop of the $k$-th ranked feature by that of the Top-1 feature:
$$\text{Decay Ratio}_k = \frac{|\text{prob\_delta}_k|}{|\text{prob\_delta}_1|}$$

## 4. Discussion & Research Findings

### Research Question Answered
> **How concentrated is a competitor's representation across SAE features?**

### Observations
1. **Flatter Decay Profiles for Semantic Competitors**:
   - Semantic competitors (e.g., `" question"`, `" doubt"`) exhibited much flatter Top-10 decay profiles than grammatical competitors. The Top-2 feature produced a probability drop that was 94.7% (for `" question"`) and 92.3% (for `" doubt"`) of the magnitude of the Top-1 feature's drop.
2. **Steeper Decay Profiles for Grammatical Competitors**:
   - Grammatical competitors (e.g., `" the"`, `" a"`, `" an"`) exhibited steep decay profiles, with subsequent features decaying quickly (dropping to ~33% - 40% of the Top-1 feature's drop).
3. **Shared Top Features**:
   - Within the discovered Top-10 influential features, every identified feature was shared by multiple competitors. Across the 18 unique features analyzed, all 18 were associated with more than one competitor.

### Interpretations
1. **Implications of Flat Decay Profiles**:
   - The relatively flat decay profiles suggest that suppressing only the highest-ranked feature may leave several similarly influential features unaffected. This pattern is consistent with the possibility that multiple influential SAE features contribute to these competitors, which may reduce the effectiveness of single-feature interventions.
2. **Implications of Steep Decay Profiles**:
   - The concentration of grammatical competitors on a single shared feature indicates that Feature 313 may play a prominent role in the representation of stopword competitors.
3. **Implications of Feature Overlap**:
   - The observation that all 18 analyzed features are shared by multiple competitors suggests that competitor tokens rely on overlapping influential SAE features rather than entirely distinct feature sets.
