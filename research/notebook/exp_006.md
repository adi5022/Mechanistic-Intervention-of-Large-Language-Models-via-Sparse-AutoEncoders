# Experiment: Hybrid Mute & Boost Steering

**Experiment ID**: EXP-006  
**Date**: 2026-07-22  
**Status**: Completed  

---

## 1. Context & Motivation
### Question
Can we overcome the grammatical/syntax competitor barrier by simultaneously muting competitor-supporting features and boosting correct target-supporting features (hybrid steering)?

### Background
In EXP-002, competitor-focused ablation successfully corrected factual competitors (Rome, Paris) but failed on generic grammatical/syntax competitors like `" the"` or `" a"`. Muting alone was like "trying to empty an ocean with a bucket" because syntax completions are supported by multiple redundant features across the network.

### Motivation
By combining competitor muting (pushing down the incorrect answer) with target feature boosting (pulling up the correct answer), we can apply a dual force to shift the token ranking.

---

## 2. Hypothesis & Setup
### Hypothesis
* **H8**: Hybrid steering (Mute & Boost applied simultaneously) is more effective at correcting factual completion errors than muting or boosting alone, especially for prompts blocked by grammatical competitors.

### Experimental Setup
* **Model**: GPT-2-small (Layer 8 Residual Stream, `blocks.8.hook_resid_pre`)
* **SAE**: `gpt2-small-res-jb`
* **Test Dataset**: Confirmed-suppressed facts from ROME knowns (specifically focusing on Dublin and Melbourne cases).
* **Intervention**:
  - Mute competitor features using `make_joint_ablation_hook` (strength = 0.2).
  - Boost target helper features using `make_signed_ablation_hook` (strength = +0.7).
  - Apply both hooks concurrently in a single forward pass.

---

## 3. Results & Observations
### Results
* **Dublin Prompt**: *"Eavan Boland was born in"* (Target: Dublin)
  - **Baseline**: Blocked by grammatical competitor `" the"`.
  - **Outcome**: Succeeded. Target reached Top-1.
* **Melbourne Prompt**: *"2005 Australian Open is located in"* (Target: Melbourne)
  - **Baseline**: Blocked by competitors.
  - **Outcome**: Succeeded. Target reached Top-1.

### Observations
The hybrid intervention succeeded on Dublin and Melbourne where all prior single-method attempts (pure muting or pure boosting) failed. This confirmed that applying a dual force (muting the competitor while boosting the target) can override grammatical biases.

### Interpretation
* **H8 is validated**: The combination of muting competitor drivers and boosting target helpers is highly effective and outperforms isolated interventions.
* However, safety remains a concern since adding positive boosting vectors can amplify competitor tokens if not properly gated, making combination safety checks essential.

---

## 4. Discussion & Next Steps
### Limitations
* Positive boosting can easily degrade model specificity if the boosted features are polysemantic.
* Manual selection of combination sizes requires search loops.

### Next Steps
* Integrate safety check gates (`check_combination_safe`) to ensure joint interventions do not introduce new blocker tokens.
* Develop the Explainable AI layer to translate these hybrid feature combinations into plain English.
