# Experiment 010: Weighted Multi-Feature Competitor Reduction Sweep

This experiment evaluates the effects of distributing competitor-focused steering across multiple causal features (Top-K per competitor) instead of muting only the single most dominant driving feature. We test three alternative feature weighting strategies to see which strategy best models distributed competitor representation.

## Experimental Parameters
- **Prompt**: `"The location of Massachusetts Institute of Technology is in"`
- **Target Completion**: `" Cambridge"` (Clean Rank: 8, Clean Prob: 1.1525%)
- **Max Mute Strength**: `0.70`
- **Sweep Matrix**:
  - **Top-K Features Per Competitor ($K$)**: `{1, 2, 3, 5}`
  - **Weighting Method**: `{"equal", "delta_normalized", "softmax"}`

---

## Observations

### 1. Regression Test ($K = 1$)
For $K = 1$, all weighting methods mathematically collapse to a single weight of $1.0$. The results were identical across all three strategies:
- **Target New Rank**: `8`
- **Target New Prob**: `1.386489%`
- **Unique Muted Features**: `2` (Feature 313 and Feature 21169)
- **Saturated Features**: `0`

This matches the Weighted Multi-Competitor Reduction (Tab 7) summation result exactly, confirming the new implementation's regression correctness.

## Interpretations & Conclusions

1. **Distributed Intervention Regains Target Rank**:
   Distributing the muting across $K = 3$ features per competitor improves steering performance compared to $K = 1$. The target token's rank recovered to **Rank 6** (from clean Rank 8 and $K=1$ Rank 8), with the target probability increasing to **1.52%** (compared to $1.15\%$ clean and $1.39\%$ at $K=1$).
   
2. **Weighting Strategy Comparison**:
   At $K = 3$, `equal` and `softmax` weighting strategies slightly outperform `delta_normalized`. This suggests that allocating strength more evenly or via a temperature-scaled softmax across active causal features is more effective than standard delta-normalization, which can over-allocate strength to a single dominant feature.
   
3. **Decay/Dilution at higher $K$**:
   At $K = 5$, performance drops back to **Rank 7** and target probability falls to **1.34%**. This dilution occurs because the intervention is spread across 13 unique features, decreasing the individual muting strength applied to the core competitor features.
