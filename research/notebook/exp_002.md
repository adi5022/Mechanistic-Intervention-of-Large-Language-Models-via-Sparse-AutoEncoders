# Experiment: Competitor-Focused Iterative Ablation

**Experiment ID**: EXP-002  
**Date**: 2026-07-20  
**Status**: Completed  

---

## 1. Context & Motivation
### Question
Can we correct factual errors in language models by iteratively muting the active features driving the model's *incorrect top competitor predictions*, rather than muting target features?

### Background
In EXP-001, targeting the correct target token's helper features resulted in a 4.55% success rate because muting the helper features simply weakened the correct prediction, making it drop further.

### Motivation
Instead of weakening the correct answer, we must suppress the incorrect competitor to let the correct factual answer emerge.

---

## 2. Hypothesis & Setup
### Hypothesis
* **H2**: Iteratively identifying and muting the top causal features driving the model's *incorrect top-1 prediction* will allow the correct target token to rise to the top-1 position.

### Experimental Setup
* **Model**: GPT-2-small (Layer 8 Residual Stream, `blocks.8.hook_resid_pre`)
* **SAE**: `gpt2-small-res-jb`
* **Test Dataset**: 22 suppressed facts from ROME's `known_1000` dataset.
* **Intervention**: Identify features driving the top incorrect prediction and apply soft-ablation hooks at strength `0.3`, repeating up to `5` rounds.

---

## 3. Results & Observations
### Results
* **Total Suppressed Facts Tested**: 22
* **Intervention Success Count**: 3 (Rome, Rome, Paris)
* **Success Rate**: 13.64% (tripled from 4.55% in EXP-001)
* **Average Rounds Needed (Successful Cases)**: 1.00
* **Average Specificity Score (Successful Cases)**: 100.00% (zero collateral damage to control prompts)

### Observations
We observed a sharp split in why some cases succeeded while others failed:
1. **Factual Competitors (Success)**: When incorrect guesses were specific facts (e.g. "London" instead of "Paris"), muting their factual features allowed the correct answer to emerge immediately.
2. **Grammatical Competitors (Failure)**: When incorrect guesses were grammatical tokens (e.g., `" the"`, `" in"`), the model's predictions resisted single-feature muting, and we exhausted all 5 rounds without shifting the rank.

---

## 4. Discussion & Next Steps
### Limitations
* Grammatical competitors cannot be resolved with single-feature iterative soft-ablation.
* A fixed ablation strength of 0.3 was too weak for some features and caused no response.

### Next Steps
* Implement hybrid mute-and-boost steering (EXP-006) to combine competitor muting with target feature amplification.
* Develop multi-feature safety checking to verify joint interventions.
