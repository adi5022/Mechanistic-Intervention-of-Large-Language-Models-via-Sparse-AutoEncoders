## 1. Context & Investigation Goal

## 3. Analysis & Key Findings

### Verification of Implementation Integrity (Option A vs B)
An independent test was run applying only the `Mute 3 | Boost 4` hooks on a clean model. It yielded the exact same probability (`6.735700%`) and rank (`2`). This proves **Option A (implementation state leak or stale hook bugs) is rejected**. The hook manager successfully resets hooks between iterations.

### Diagnosis of Regressions
The regressions are explained by **Option B: Expected mathematical consequence of softmax probabilities and ranks interacting**:

1. **Rank Improvement with Absolute Probability Decrease**:
   - In `Mute 2 | Boost 3` (Prob = 4.282001%, Rank = 4) vs. `Mute 5 | Boost 3` (Prob = 3.796710%, Rank = 3):
     - The target's absolute probability decreased, but its rank improved from 4 to 3.
     - Under `Mute 2`, the competitor token `" question"` was left untouched and sat at `6.1357%`, keeping the target at Rank 4. Under `Mute 5`, the additional ablated features successfully suppressed the features driving `" question"`, dropping it to `2.2965%` (below the target's `3.7967%`), thereby raising the target's relative rank.
2. **Rank & Probability Regression when Boosting More Features**:
   - In `Mute 3 | Boost 3` (Prob = 7.326410%, Rank = 1) vs. `Mute 3 | Boost 4` (Prob = 6.735700%, Rank = 2):
     - When adding the 4th target-boosting feature (Feature 24181), the target probability decreased, and `" the"` reclaimed Rank 1.
     - Feature 24181 is polysemantic; it amplifies `" the"` (`6.78%` -> `7.04%`) more than it helps `" Cambridge"` (`7.33%` -> `6.74%`). Due to the softmax partition function, the relative probability of `" Cambridge"` was suppressed, causing the rank to regress.
