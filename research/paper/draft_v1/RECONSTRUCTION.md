## 4. Rejected, supported, and open hypotheses (as supported by evidence)

- H1 (mute the target's own helper features): rejected (1 of 22).
- H2 (mute the competitor's features): improved (3 of 22); limited to a few rank-2 facts.
- H10 (sum rather than max for shared features): rejected on the MIT prompt.
- H11 (competitor support spread over several features): supported by one-prompt observations only. Not a general claim.
- H12 (top-K muting): rank 8 to 6 at K=3 on one prompt; one run per setting.
- H4/H5, H6 to H9: partly documented; H6 to H8 cite `conversations.json`, which is not in the repo (no raw output). H9 rests on the printed sweep in exp_007.
- Candidate source (Entry 20): all positions better than last token on 13 comparable prompts (10 vs 4 reach rank 1).
- Safety filter (Entry 21): removing it hurts (pilot and full); relaxing it does not help (pilot 0, full at most 1 new prompt).

## 7. Discrepancies found

1. **Fact bank size.** Journals and the old draft say 46; `src/fact_bank.py` has 45 and 15 + 8 + 22 = 45. Re-running `classify_fact` reproduced 15 / 8 / 22.
2. **Stage-1 single success.** Entries 1 and 2 and `baseline_target_report.md` narrate the Eiffel Tower prompt as the single variant-A success. The result table lists Francesco Castellacci (Rome), and the fact bank has no Eiffel Tower entry.
3. **Factual vs grammatical competitors.** `report.md` claims a 37.5% success rate for "pure factual competitors (Rome, Rome, Paris, ...)". `results.csv` shows the three successes had initial top-1 tokens "Italy", "P", "May", so this statistic cannot be reproduced from the table.
4. **Entry 8 title / H label.** Entry 8 says "H11 sweep"; the hypothesis log and notebook exp_010 call it H12.
5. **Forward-pass counts.** Entry 14 header says 126 to 6; its table and Step 3A say 122 to 6.
6. **Hardware and software.** Entry 12: GTX 1660 Ti, torch 2.6.0+cu124. Entry 13: RTX 4050 Laptop GPU, torch 2.5.1+cu121. Entry 15: not stated. `requirements.txt` pins torch 2.13.0.
7. **`benchmark_test_prompts.json`.** Entry 12 calls it 22 suppressed facts from `fact_bank.py`; in the 21-prompt run, 9 prompts had the target at rank 1. `datasets/rome_22_prompts.json` is a different 22-prompt set.
8. **Success flag.** The layer benchmark's `success` is "rank OR probability improved". Entry 10's table shows "Success: No" while the stored artifact has `success: true`. Entry 10 values match the artifact to within 0.01 pp (0.79/2.37/0.82 vs 0.78/2.38/0.81).
9. **Bibliography errors.** `research/bibliography.bib` has AxBench as arXiv 2501.07727; the abstract page verified for the paper is 2501.17148. Entry 12 titles the Bricken paper "Global Explanations in a Small Model"; the PDF's title is "Decomposing Language Models With Dictionary Learning". Entry 12's descriptions of what Bricken, Cunningham, and Templeton "formalized" about delta patching are not verified (only Bricken's PDF is in the repo, and it was not found to state that).
10. **Weighted reduction default.** `run_weighted_multi_competitor_reduction` implements the additive H10 variant. The max-rule results (1.29%) cannot be regenerated without changing the code.
11. **Chained hooks.** `iterative_ablate` and `check_specificity` stack one hook per feature and hard-code `blocks.8.hook_resid_pre`.
12. **Entry 5 sweep table.** Its "Mute 3 | Boost 3, rank 1" row uses unfiltered candidates (exp_007 shows the safe-only rows reach rank 2). Entry 5 does not say so.
13. **architecture.md** contains an unverified novelty statement (a three-way comparison "the existing literature does not provide") and mentions of other papers (SAFE, SALVE 2025) that were not checked.

## 8. Decisions I made that you may want to reverse

1. The paper is centred on the current pipeline (hybrid mute and boost, filter, refill, candidate source) and treats the older weighted-reduction experiments as one-prompt development experiments. The removed app tabs are not described.
2. The stage-1 experiments (H1/H2) are reported from the result tables, not the narratives.
3. Statistics are from the existing analysis code (exact sign and McNemar), uncorrected, and stated as such.
4. Author block and year of the GPT-2 report are placeholders.
